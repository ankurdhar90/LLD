09 Producing the NSFR, end to end

Purpose: Give you a complete, step-by-step procedure for producing Example Bank's monthly Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR), from the first data extract to the signed working paper, the ALCO (asset and liability committee) pack and the feed to the APRA return.

You will be able to:

  • Run the monthly NSFR timetable and say what must be true at each step before you move on.
  • Classify every liability and equity line into the right available stable funding (ASF) category, and every asset and off-balance sheet item into the right required stable funding (RSF) category, using the decision tables in this document.
  • Build the full NSFR working paper for Example Bank and reproduce 136.3 per cent exactly.
  • Reconcile the NSFR inputs to the general ledger and run reasonableness checks that catch errors before a reviewer does.
  • Explain a month-on-month movement line by line, and forecast the NSFR for the coming months.
  • Review a colleague's NSFR using the checklist, and prepare the sign-off and ALCO report.

Before you start: Read document 02 (Liquidity from zero) for the idea of a balance sheet and the first narrated NSFR; document 06 (APS 210 companion: Attachments B and C) for the rules, paragraph by paragraph; and document 07 (Data: where the numbers come from and how each balance is classified) for the source systems and data fields. Document 08 (Producing the daily LCR) is useful for comparison but is not required.

Time: About 2 hours to read; about 6 hours to work through once with the Example Bank data; about 1 working day each month to produce the real thing (Example Bank convention; typical practice; confirm with your controller).

1. What you are producing and why

The NSFR asks one question: over the next year, does the bank have enough steady, dependable funding to support the assets it holds and the promises it has made? (Att C para 1). The standard answers it with a ratio:

NSFR = Available stable funding (ASF) / Required stable funding (RSF)

(Att C para 3). The minimum for a locally incorporated LCR ADI (liquidity coverage ratio authorised deposit-taking institution) is 100 per cent (APS 210 paras 59 and 60). An ADI must also hold a buffer above that minimum "at all times" in line with its risk tolerance (Att C para 4).

  • ASF is each funding source (a deposit, a bond, equity) multiplied by a percentage showing how reliably it will stay for one year (Att C paras 6 and 7).
  • RSF is each asset or promise multiplied by a percentage showing how much stable funding it ties up (Att C paras 16 and 17).

Example Bank is a single legal entity in a single currency, so Level 1 (the bank alone) and Level 2 (the bank and its subsidiaries together) are the same here. The figures in this document are in A$ billions (A$bn).

Example Bank's position, at a glance: ASF 79.75; RSF 58.50; NSFR 136.3 per cent. Board risk appetite is 110 per cent and the amber early-warning trigger is 115 per cent (both are Example Bank conventions; typical practice; confirm with your controller). They are not in APS 210.

From the controller: The NSFR moves slowly compared with the LCR, which is why we produce it monthly rather than daily. But it can still move sharply when a big funding line crosses the six-month or one-year boundary, or when someone pledges a large asset for a long time. Most of your work is making sure each line is in the right bucket, so spend your time on classification.

5. The ten steps

Step 1: Load the data

  1. Confirm the ledger has closed and the trial balance balances.
  2. Load each frozen extract into the NSFR working area: one tab per source (deposits, wholesale debt, loans, securities, repos and securities financing, derivatives, collateral, facilities, trade finance).
  3. For each extract, record: source system, extract date and time, row count and control total. Compare with the source system's own control total.

Control: Row counts and totals agree to the source. If they do not, stop and ask the system owner.

Step 2: Check completeness of attributes

For every line, check that each attribute in section 4 is present and valid (a customer type from the approved list, a maturity date that is a real date on or after month-end, an encumbrance flag of yes or no).

When an attribute is missing, take the conservative category and record it. Conservative means the one that makes the NSFR lower:

  • for a liability, the lower ASF factor;
  • for an asset, the higher RSF factor.

Record each case in the exception log with the item, the missing attribute, the treatment applied, and who is chasing the data. For example, a corporate deposit with no operational flag is treated as non-operational (50 per cent either way) but a retail deposit with no stable flag is treated as less stable (90 per cent, not 95).

Step 3: Reconcile to the general ledger

Compare totals from the extracts with the ledger:

Check Rule
Total assets in the NSFR data = total assets in the ledger Difference must be nil, or explained and within the tolerance you have been told (typical practice: nil for the total, with timing items explained individually; confirm with your controller).
Total liabilities plus equity in the NSFR data = total liabilities plus equity in the ledger As above.
Total assets = total liabilities plus equity Must hold. If the ledger balances but the data does not, one extract is incomplete.
Each product total agrees to its ledger account group Differences logged and explained.

Section 9.1 has a template. Never plug a difference into "other assets" or "other liabilities" to force agreement.

Step 4: Classify liabilities and equity into ASF categories

Use the decision table in section 6. Record, for each line, the category, the factor and the paragraph.

Step 5: Classify assets into RSF categories

Use the decision table in section 7. Apply the encumbrance rule (Att C para 23) before you choose the final factor.

Step 6: Classify off-balance sheet exposures

Apply Att C Table 1 to undrawn facilities, and Att C para 42 to trade finance, guarantees and letters of credit. Add the derivative items (section 7.4).

Step 7: Calculate

For each line: weighted amount = carrying value x factor. Sum the ASF lines to get total ASF (Att C para 7). Sum the RSF lines to get total RSF (Att C para 17). Then:

NSFR = total ASF / total RSF

Round the ratio to one decimal place for reporting. Keep the working paper unrounded.

Checks on the calculation: (a) re-add the totals by a second method (for example, a pivot by factor); (b) check that every weighted amount is between zero and the carrying value; (c) check the ratio against a calculator.

Step 8: Reasonableness and movement checks

Sections 10 and 11 set out the checks and the movement analysis.

Step 9: Independent review

The reviewer uses the checklist in section 13, re-performs a sample of classifications and recalculates the totals. Questions go back to the preparer in writing and are cleared before sign-off.

Step 10: Sign-off, report and archive

  1. The controller signs the working paper.
  2. Send the result to Liquidity Risk (independent oversight, APS 210 para 28).
  3. Issue the ALCO pack (section 14).
  4. Archive the frozen data, the working paper, the review checklist, the exception log and the sign-off.
  5. Make the figures available for the APRA return (section 15).

6. Classifying liabilities and equity (ASF)

6.1 The order of questions

Ask these questions in this order and stop at the first "yes".

  1. Is it regulatory capital, or a capital instrument? Regulatory capital before regulatory adjustments (excluding Tier 2 under one year) gets 100 per cent (Att C para 11(a)). Another capital instrument with effective residual maturity of one year or more, with no option that could shorten it below one year, gets 100 per cent (Att C para 11(b)).
  2. Is the residual maturity one year or more? Residual maturity for funding is measured to the earliest date the funds may be redeemed (Att C para 8). Any borrowing, liability or term deposit with an effective residual maturity of one year or more gets 100 per cent, whoever provided it (Att C para 11(c)). Only the part of the cash flows falling on or after the one-year point counts.
  3. Is it a retail or SME deposit with residual maturity under one year? Stable gets 95 per cent (Att C para 12(a)); less stable gets 90 per cent (Att C para 13(a)). Use the same stable or less stable split as the LCR (Att A para 37; Att A para 38).
  4. Is it an intermediated deposit? Test the conditions in Att A para 35 first. If they are met: 95 per cent for stable with at least 12 months' notice (Att C para 12(b)); 90 per cent for less stable with at least 12 months' notice (Att C para 13(b)); 50 per cent where the intermediary needs at least six months' notice (Att C para 14(d)). If the conditions are not met, the deposit is treated as from a financial institution (go to question 7).
  5. Is it an operational deposit? 50 per cent, including superannuation fund deposits that meet the operational definition (Att C para 14(b) and (c)). The operational tests are in Att A paras 47 to 50.
  6. Is it funding under one year from a non-financial corporate, or a sovereign, PSE or MDB? 50 per cent (Att C para 14(a) and (e)).
  7. Is it funding from a financial institution or a central bank? Residual maturity six months to under one year: 50 per cent (Att C para 14(f)). Under six months: zero (Att C para 15(a)).
  8. Is it a liability with no stated maturity, a derivative liability, or a trade date payable? Zero (Att C para 15(b), (c) and (d)), except deferred tax liabilities and minority interests, which follow their effective maturity: 100 per cent if one year or more, 50 per cent if six months to under one year (Att C para 15(b)).
  9. Anything else: zero (Att C para 15(a)).

6.3 The maturity buckets and how to measure them

Bucket Meaning for funding Where it matters
Under 6 months Earliest redemption date is within 6 months of the reporting date Financial institution and central bank funding scores 0 (Att C para 15(a))
6 months to under 1 year Earliest redemption date is 6 months or more, but less than 1 year, away Financial institution funding scores 50 per cent (Att C para 14(f))
1 year or more Earliest redemption date is 1 year or more away 100 per cent (Att C para 11(c))

Rules to apply:

  • Earliest redemption date (Att C para 8). For a bond the holder can put back to us after nine months, the maturity is nine months, not the five-year final maturity. For a deposit with a 31-day notice period, the maturity is 31 days from the reporting date at the earliest.
  • Cash flows split (Att C para 8). For a long-dated liability that pays in instalments, only the cash flows falling on or after the six-month and one-year points count as six months or more, and one year or more, respectively. Cash flows due in less than one year from a longer liability do not get 100 per cent (Att C para 11(c)).
  • Term deposits. A retail term deposit with eight months to run is under one year (90 per cent if less stable). The LCR's "no early withdrawal" feature (Att A paras 41 and 42) does not change the NSFR factor; the NSFR depends on residual maturity.

6.4 Special points

  • Carrying value, not the regulatory number. Use the balance sheet amount before regulatory deductions or filters (Att C footnote 9).
  • Tier 2 instruments with under one year to maturity are excluded from the 100 per cent for regulatory capital (Att C para 11(a)). They then fall into the maturity table above (financial institution or other holder and bucket).
  • Cooperative network deposits have a special rule that does not apply to Example Bank (Att C footnote 18).

7. Classifying assets and off-balance sheet items (RSF)

7.1 The order of questions

Ask these in order and stop at the first "yes".

  1. Is the asset on our balance sheet and ours? Exclude borrowed securities where we have no beneficial ownership (for example, securities received under a reverse repo or collateral swap that are not on our balance sheet). Include securities lent or repo'd where we keep beneficial ownership (Att C para 25). Include purchases that have been executed but not yet settled; sales executed may be excluded if the conditions are met (Att C para 19).
  2. Is it encumbered? Apply the encumbrance rule (section 7.2) and use the result as a floor if it gives a higher factor than the asset's own category.
  3. Is it cash or an RBA balance, or a trade date receivable? Zero (Att C para 29).
  4. Is it unencumbered HQLA1, or an equivalent sovereign security? 5 per cent (Att C para 30).
  5. Is it a short loan to a financial institution? Under six months: 10 per cent if secured by freely re-usable HQLA1 (Att C para 31), otherwise 15 per cent (Att C para 33(b)). Six months to under one year: 50 per cent (Att C para 34(c)). One year or more: 100 per cent (Att C para 37(c)).
  6. Is it HQLA2A? 15 per cent. HQLA2B? 50 per cent (Att C paras 33(a) and 34(a)). (No Australian dollar asset currently qualifies as HQLA2, so you will rarely use these for Example Bank.)
  7. Is it a loan or other non-HQLA asset with residual maturity under one year (not a financial institution)? 50 per cent (Att C para 34(f)).
  8. Is it a standard residential mortgage to a natural person, LVR 80 per cent or below, one year or more? 65 per cent (Att C para 35(a)). Another loan of one year or more with a risk weight of 35 per cent or lower, not to a financial institution: 65 per cent (Att C para 35(b)).
  9. Is it a performing loan of one year or more that does not qualify at 65 per cent, or a non-HQLA security of one year or more, or initial margin or a default fund contribution, or a physical commodity? 85 per cent (Att C para 36).
  10. Anything else, including non-performing loans, fixed assets, goodwill, intangibles, non-exchange-traded equities: 100 per cent (Att C para 37(c)).

7.2 Encumbrance: the rule from Att C para 23

Find the assets that are pledged, backing covered bonds or securitisations, posted as collateral, or otherwise tied up (Att C footnote 21). Take the remaining encumbrance period and apply:

Remaining encumbrance period RSF factor
Less than 6 months Same as an equivalent unencumbered asset
6 months to under 1 year, and the unencumbered factor is 50% or lower 50%
6 months to under 1 year, and the unencumbered factor is above 50% The higher unencumbered factor
1 year or more 100% (Att C para 37(a))

Worked example. Example Bank pledges A$2.0bn of its Australian Government bonds (unencumbered factor 5 per cent):

Remaining pledge Factor RSF Change from unencumbered (0.10)
14 days 5% 0.10 0.00
9 months 50% 1.00 +0.90
2 years 100% 2.00 +1.90

Encumbrance can move the ratio noticeably. In the 9-month case, total RSF would move from 58.50 to 59.40 and the NSFR from 136.3 per cent to 134.3 per cent (79.75 / 59.40 = 1.3426), looking at this effect alone and ignoring any funding raised by the repo itself.

Watch out: The rule uses the remaining period of the encumbrance, not the asset's own maturity. A ten-year bond pledged for 14 days is "less than six months". Also, a repo that is routinely rolled needs judgement: if the bank expects to keep rolling it, ask the controller whether to measure the term of the arrangement; treat the doubt conservatively (typical practice; confirm with your controller).

7.4 Derivatives and the off-balance sheet items

Derivatives (the Example Bank base case ignores them; the full rule is below).

  1. Take the replacement cost (current market value) of each contract. Negative values are liabilities; positive values are assets. Net within eligible netting sets (Att C paras 9 and 27).
  2. NSFR derivative liabilities = derivative liabilities minus variation margin posted (Att C para 10; Att C footnote 11). Do not also count the variation margin asset in RSF (Att C footnote 12).
  3. NSFR derivative assets = positive replacement cost, not reduced by collateral received unless it is qualifying cash variation margin in the same currency of settlement (Att C para 28). Leftover collateral liabilities and any initial margin received get zero ASF (Att C para 28).
  4. If NSFR derivative liabilities exceed NSFR derivative assets, the excess gets zero ASF (Att C para 15(c)). If assets exceed liabilities, the excess gets 100 per cent RSF (Att C para 37(b); Att C footnote 29).
  5. Add 20 per cent of derivative liabilities, before deducting variation margin posted, to RSF (Att C para 37(d)).

Worked example (invented numbers, A$bn): derivative liabilities after netting 2.0; variation margin posted 0.5; derivative assets 1.2.

  • NSFR derivative liabilities = 2.0 - 0.5 = 1.5.
  • 1.5 is greater than 1.2, so no 100 per cent RSF on the net asset (Att C para 37(b)) and the excess 0.3 gets zero ASF (Att C para 15(c)).
  • RSF add-on = 20% x 2.0 = 0.40 (Att C para 37(d)).
  • RSF contribution from derivatives = 0.40.

If instead derivative assets were 1.8: NSFR derivative assets 1.8 minus NSFR derivative liabilities 1.5 = 0.3 at 100% = 0.30, plus 0.40, giving 0.70.

Undrawn facilities. Use Att C Table 1: irrevocable and conditionally revocable credit and liquidity facilities to any client 5 per cent; unconditionally revocable 1 per cent (Att C para 41). In the NSFR the rate does not depend on whether the customer is retail, corporate or a financial institution. When unsure whether a facility can be cancelled unconditionally, use 5 per cent and record the doubt.

Trade finance, guarantees, letters of credit. 100 per cent of the actual net outflows over the most recent 12 months (Att C para 42). Example: if trade finance has net outflows of 0.02 each month, the 12-month total is 0.24 and RSF is 0.24. (In the LCR the corresponding treatment uses different rules; do not copy the LCR figure across.)

7.5 Other points

  • Maturity of assets is measured to the latest possible date, assuming any extension option is exercised (Att C para 20). If there is no defined maturity, or maturity is under periodic review, treat the residual maturity as one year or more (Att C para 21). An amortising loan may be split so that the part due within one year is included in the under one year category (Att C para 22); check with the controller whether Example Bank does this.
  • HQLA for the NSFR is determined before the LCR caps and operational requirements (Att C footnote 20). An asset that the LCR cap stops us counting still gets its HQLA factor in the NSFR.
  • Non-maturity reverse repos take 100 per cent unless APRA agrees otherwise (Att C footnote 28).
  • Interdependent assets and liabilities are only treated as zero where APRA has made a determination (Att C paras 38 to 40). We never apply this ourselves.
  • Committed Liquidity Facility (CLF). The 10 per cent factor for CLF-eligible assets applies only up to the CLF amount (Att C para 32). The aggregate CLF was phased out to zero as at 1 January 2023, so for Example Bank the line is nil; confirm it each month.

Assumptions recorded on the working paper:

  • The short wholesale debt lines (A10, A11, A12) are held by financial institutions and the market generally, not by non-financial corporates, sovereigns, PSEs or MDBs. If a holder were a non-financial corporate, funding under one year would score 50 per cent rather than 0 per cent (Att C para 14(a)).
  • A5: the "no early withdrawal" feature matters for the LCR, not the NSFR factor, which depends on residual maturity under one year (Att C para 13(a)).
  • A8 is assumed to have residual maturity under one year (Att C para 14(a)).
  • No Tier 2 instrument has under one year to run, and regulatory capital equals the equity line (Att C para 11(a)).

11. Month-on-month movement analysis

11.1 Method

  1. Place last month's and this month's working papers side by side, line by line.
  2. For each line, calculate the change in carrying value, the change in factor (if the classification moved) and the change in weighted amount.
  3. Total the changes: change in ASF and change in RSF.
  4. Explain the ratio change by recalculating in two steps: first change only ASF, then change RSF. This shows how much each side contributed.
  5. For each large change ask: what transaction caused this, who knows (usually Treasury), and is it permanent?
  6. Record the explanation in plain language, with the paragraph if a factor changed.

11.2 Worked example: lending cash as mortgages

Example Bank lends A$3.0bn of its cash as new standard residential mortgages (LVR 80 per cent or below, one year or more).

Line Last month This month Change in carrying value Factor Change in weighted amount
B1 Cash and RBA balances 5.0 2.0 -3.0 0% (Att C para 29) 0.00
B5 Residential mortgages 50.0 53.0 +3.0 65% (Att C para 35(a)) +1.95
All ASF lines 79.75 79.75 0.0 various 0.00
  • Total RSF: 58.50 + 1.95 = 60.45.
  • Total ASF: 79.75 (no liability changed).
  • NSFR: 79.75 / 60.45 = 1.3193 = 131.9%.
  • Change in the ratio: 136.3% to 131.9%, a fall of about 4.4 percentage points. The whole fall comes from RSF: ASF contributed nothing.

Plain-language explanation for the pack: "The NSFR fell 4.4 percentage points because A$3.0bn of cash, which needs no stable funding, was lent as mortgages, which need 65 per cent. RSF rose by 1.95. Funding was unchanged. The ratio remains above the 110 per cent Board appetite and the 115 per cent amber trigger."

Compare the LCR effect of the same transaction in document 08: the LCR falls to 121.2 per cent, because the cash was part of the liquid asset buffer. The NSFR was affected through the 65 per cent factor, not through the loss of liquid assets.

13. Review checklist

The reviewer ticks each item and initials the sheet. If an item fails, return the working paper to the preparer.

Data

  • [ ] The ledger had closed before the extract was taken.
  • [ ] Every extract has a row count and control total agreed to the source.
  • [ ] Total assets and total liabilities and equity agree to the ledger and to each other.
  • [ ] Every attribute needed is present, or the exception log shows the conservative treatment.

Classification

  • [ ] The mapping table version is the approved version; any change has an approval.
  • [ ] A sample of at least 10 lines (including the five largest in each of ASF and RSF) has been re-classified independently and agrees.
  • [ ] Residual maturity for liabilities uses the earliest redemption date (Att C para 8).
  • [ ] Residual maturity for assets uses the latest possible date (Att C para 20).
  • [ ] Stable and less stable tags agree to the daily LCR.
  • [ ] Encumbered assets have been identified and the encumbrance rule applied (Att C para 23).
  • [ ] Securities financing: beneficial ownership checked (Att C para 25).
  • [ ] Derivative lines calculated, or the exclusion is explained (Att C paras 9, 10, 27, 28 and 37).
  • [ ] Facilities classified by revocability (Att C Table 1); trade finance and guarantees use 12-month actual net outflows (Att C para 42).

Calculation

  • [ ] Each weighted amount equals the carrying value times the factor.
  • [ ] Totals re-added by a second method.
  • [ ] The ratio recomputed independently.

Reasonableness and movement

  • [ ] Reasonableness checks completed (section 10).
  • [ ] Every movement over tolerance explained with a cause.
  • [ ] Result compared with the minimum, the Board appetite and the amber trigger.

Sign-off

  • [ ] Questions cleared in writing.
  • [ ] Working paper saved and locked; version recorded.
  • [ ] Controller has signed.

14. Sign-off, reporting to ALCO and escalation

14.1 Sign-off

The four-eyes rule applies: the preparer signs that the working paper is complete and correct to the best of their knowledge; the reviewer signs that they have performed the checklist; the controller signs that the figure may be released. Each signature is dated. The sign-off record is part of the evidence.

14.2 The ALCO NSFR report

Include (typical practice; confirm with your controller):

  1. The NSFR, ASF and RSF, this month and the last six months.
  2. Comparison with the 100 per cent minimum, the 110 per cent Board appetite and the 115 per cent amber trigger.
  3. The movement analysis in plain language, with the three largest drivers.
  4. The funding mix: ASF by category.
  5. A note on the largest RSF lines and any change in encumbrance.
  6. The forecast for the next three to twelve months (section 16) with the key assumptions.
  7. Exceptions and judgements: missing attributes, manual overrides, items awaiting APRA determinations.
  8. Actions proposed or in progress.

15. How the NSFR feeds the APRA return

The NSFR is reported to APRA under Reporting Standard ARS 210.0 Liquidity. In the April 2021 version of that standard, the NSFR form is ARF 210.6; the in-force version may differ, and document 11 (Returns to APRA and public disclosure) confirms the current form, its content, the reporting basis and the due dates. Do not rely on this document for those details.

What you do each reporting period:

  1. Take the signed NSFR figures at the reporting date from the working paper. Do not recalculate them for the return; use the signed numbers.
  2. Map each ASF and RSF category to the cell on the form that collects it (document 11 has the mapping table).
  3. Reconcile the return totals (total ASF, total RSF and the ratio) to the working paper, line by line.
  4. Reconcile the return's balance sheet totals to the general ledger.
  5. If the return and the working paper differ, find the cause before submission. If an error is found in the working paper after sign-off, follow document 12 and tell the controller at once.

APS 210 does not set the due dates, so do not state any from memory. Use the calendar in document 11.

16. Forecasting the NSFR

A forecast tells the controller and ALCO whether the NSFR will stay above the Board appetite. The standard requires a buffer "at all times" (Att C para 4), and a monthly figure alone cannot show what happens between month-ends.

16.1 Method

  1. Start from the signed position at the last month-end.
  2. Roll the maturity profile forward. For every liability and asset, work out its residual maturity at each future date. Funding crossing the one-year and six-month boundaries changes factor (Att C paras 8 and 11 to 15). Assets maturing out of the book drop out; assets nearing maturity may change factor (Att C paras 20 to 22).
  3. Add planned business. Use the budget or Treasury's plans for loan growth (mortgages, business loans), deposit growth and wholesale issuance and repayments. Ask Treasury for the funding plan (document 10 covers the three-year funding strategy).
  4. Add planned encumbrance changes: new repos, covered bond issues, securitisations (Att C para 23).
  5. Classify each future line with the same decision tables as for actuals.
  6. Calculate projected ASF, RSF and NSFR for each month.
  7. Test against limits and run a downside: for example, deposits growing 2 per cent slower, or no wholesale issuance.
  8. Document the assumptions and compare the forecast to the actual next month. Explain differences.

16.2 Worked mini-forecast

Start: ASF 79.75; RSF 58.50; NSFR 136.3 per cent.

Step What happens ASF RSF NSFR
Today Signed position 79.75 58.50 136.3%
1 A$3.0bn of the A$15.0bn long-dated debt falls to 11 months residual: ASF -1.50 78.25 58.50 133.8%
2 Treasury issues A$3.0bn of 5-year debt: ASF +3.00 (and the cash received sits at 0 per cent RSF) 81.25 58.50 138.9%
3 Mortgages grow A$4.0bn, funded from the cash raised in step 2: RSF +4.0 x 65% = +2.60, no change in ASF 81.25 61.10 133.0%

Checks: 78.25 / 58.50 = 1.3376; 81.25 / 58.50 = 1.3889; 81.25 / 61.10 = 1.3298.

Notice the pattern: the ratio is driven by the maturity of funding and the growth of long assets. In the forecast, the question to ask is whether the funding plan keeps pace with lending.

16.3 What to give ALCO

The monthly projections, the lowest point, the month it occurs, the assumptions, and the levers available (issuing term debt, slowing lending, replacing wholesale funding with retail term deposits). Levers are decisions for Treasury and ALCO, not for Liquidity Reporting.

Check yourself

  1. What is the NSFR formula and the regulatory minimum for a locally incorporated LCR ADI? Answer: NSFR = ASF / RSF (Att C para 3). The minimum is 100 per cent (APS 210 paras 59 and 60).

  2. What ASF factor applies to A$25.0bn of stable retail deposits at call, and what is the weighted amount? Answer: 95 per cent (Att C para 12(a)). 25.0 x 0.95 = 23.75.

  3. Total ASF is 72.0 and total RSF is 60.0. What is the NSFR, and is it above a 110 per cent Board appetite? Answer: 72.0 / 60.0 = 1.20 = 120.0 per cent. Yes, 120.0 per cent is above 110 per cent.

  4. Example Bank pledges A$2.0bn of Australian Government bonds under a repo that has 8 months left. What RSF factor applies, what is the RSF, and by how much does RSF rise compared with unencumbered? Answer: Remaining encumbrance is 6 months to under 1 year and the unencumbered factor (5 per cent) is 50 per cent or lower, so 50 per cent applies (Att C para 23). RSF = 2.0 x 50% = 1.00. Unencumbered it was 2.0 x 5% = 0.10. The increase is 0.90.

  5. Why do deposits from other banks with under six months to run get zero ASF, while the same type of deposit from a non-financial corporate with under one year to run gets 50 per cent? Answer: The standard treats funding from financial institutions as less reliable than funding from non-financial corporates, because financial institutions are likelier to withdraw in stress. Funding under six months from financial institutions gets zero (Att C para 15(a)); funding under one year from non-financial corporates gets 50 per cent (Att C para 14(a)).

  6. A A$1.5bn loan to another bank has 5 months left but the bank has an option to extend it to 14 months. What is the RSF? Answer: Assets are measured to the latest possible date and an extension option is assumed to be exercised (Att C para 20). 14 months is one year or more, and loans to financial institutions of one year or more take 100 per cent (Att C para 37(c)). RSF = 1.5 x 100% = 1.50. Treated as under 6 months it would have been 15 per cent, or 0.225.

  7. Example Bank lends A$3.0bn of cash as standard mortgages. Calculate the new NSFR. Answer: Cash at 0 per cent loses nothing; mortgages gain 3.0 x 65% = 1.95. RSF = 58.50 + 1.95 = 60.45. ASF unchanged at 79.75. NSFR = 79.75 / 60.45 = 131.9 per cent.

  8. The ledger shows total assets of 100.0 and total liabilities and equity of 100.0, but your extracts show assets of 100.0 and liabilities and equity of 100.2. What do you do? Answer: Stop. Do not plug the 0.2. Find which liability extract is duplicated or mis-mapped, agree each product to the ledger, fix the data, and record the cause. Tell the controller if it cannot be resolved within the timetable.

  9. Derivative liabilities after netting are 2.0, variation margin posted is 0.5, and derivative assets are 1.2. What are NSFR derivative liabilities and the RSF add-on? Answer: NSFR derivative liabilities = 2.0 - 0.5 = 1.5 (Att C para 10). The add-on is 20 per cent of derivative liabilities before deducting variation margin: 20% x 2.0 = 0.40 (Att C para 37(d)). Because 1.5 exceeds the derivative assets of 1.2, there is no 100 per cent charge under Att C para 37(b).

  10. Example Bank replaces A$2.0bn of at-call financial institution deposits with A$2.0bn of retail term deposits (under 1 year, less stable). What is the new NSFR, and the headroom over the 110 per cent Board appetite in ASF terms? Answer: ASF rises from 2.0 x 0% to 2.0 x 90%, a change of +1.80, so ASF = 81.55 and NSFR = 81.55 / 58.50 = 139.4 per cent. Headroom over 110 per cent: 81.55 - (58.50 x 1.10) = 81.55 - 64.35 = 17.20.

What to remember

  • The NSFR is ASF divided by RSF, with a minimum of 100 per cent (Att C para 3; APS 210 paras 59 and 60). Example Bank is at 136.3 per cent: 79.75 over 58.50.
  • Funding is measured to the earliest date it can leave; assets to the latest date they can come back (Att C paras 8 and 20).
  • Retail and SME deposits are 95 per cent (stable) or 90 per cent (less stable); financial institution funding under six months gets zero; equity and funding of one year or more get 100 per cent.
  • Mortgages (standard, LVR 80 per cent or below, one year or more) take 65 per cent, other long performing loans 85 per cent, and other assets 100 per cent.
  • Encumbrance raises the RSF factor; classify it before you pick the final factor (Att C para 23).
  • Reconcile to the ledger first. Total assets must equal total liabilities and equity, and neither may be plugged.
  • Explain every movement with a transaction, a line and a paragraph. A ratio can fall because funding moves closer to maturity, not only because of new business.
  • Missing data means the conservative category, recorded in the exception log.
  • The four-eyes rule applies: you prepare, a colleague reviews, the controller signs. Only then does the number go to ALCO and into the APRA return.
