11 Returns to APRA and public disclosure

Purpose: To teach you how the bank reports its liquidity position to APRA and to the public: what a regulatory return is, which forms Example Bank files and when, how each part of the LCR and NSFR calculation lands on a form, how we produce, check, sign and submit a return, which notifications and approvals APS 210 requires outside the returns, and how public disclosure under APS 330 works.

1. What a regulatory return is

1.1 In everyday words

Every year you may send a tax return to the tax office. It is a form, with set boxes, filled in from your records, signed to say it is true, and sent by a due date. A regulatory return is the same idea for a bank. APRA (the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority) is the regulator that licenses and supervises banks. It cannot supervise what it cannot see. So it requires every bank to send numbers in a standard layout, on a regular schedule.

Three things make a return different from an internal report:

  • Fixed definitions. Every bank must put the same thing in the same box. That is what lets APRA compare banks.
  • A legal due date. Missing it is a breach, not an inconvenience.
  • Accountability. Someone senior has to be able to stand behind the numbers, and the controls behind them are tested by the external auditor.

1.2 Why APRA wants liquidity data

APRA says it uses the data "for the purpose of prudential supervision, including assessing compliance with Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity" and that it "may also be used by the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA)" (ARS 210.0 clause 2, verified). In practice, the liquidity returns let APRA:

  • check that each bank meets the LCR (APS 210 para 55) and the NSFR (APS 210 para 60) at quarter end;
  • see the shape of each bank's maturity mismatch and funding plan;
  • compare banks, spot trends and find banks that need a closer look;
  • during a crisis, ask for the daily liquidity report.

From the controller: The numbers on the form are not a second set of numbers. They are the same numbers as the daily LCR and the monthly NSFR, laid out in APRA's boxes. If they differ, one of them is wrong, and APRA can compare us with everybody else.

2. Prudential standards, reporting standards and forms

APRA issues several kinds of document. New staff often mix them up, so here is the family.

Document type What it is Made under Example
Prudential standard Rules a bank must follow (what to hold, how to manage risk) Section 11AF of the Banking Act 1959 (APS 210 para 1) APS 210 Liquidity
Prudential practice guide APRA's guidance on how to apply a standard; not a rule Not binding APG 210 Liquidity
Reporting standard Rules on what data must be sent to APRA, when and how Section 13 of the Financial Sector (Collection of Data) Act 2001 (ARS 210.0 clause 1, verified) ARS 210.0 Liquidity
Reporting form (ARF) The numbered form inside a reporting standard, with instructions Part of the reporting standard ARF 210.1A
Public disclosure standard Rules on what the bank must publish Section 11AF of the Banking Act 1959 APS 330 Public Disclosure

Notes:

  • "ADI" means authorised deposit-taking institution, the legal name for a bank (and some building societies and credit unions) licensed under the Banking Act 1959.
  • A prudential standard says "you must hold a 100% LCR". A reporting standard says "tell APRA your LCR by this date in this layout".
  • APS 210 does not set reporting frequency or due dates. A line in APS 210 says it applies to all ADIs; the returns and the timing are set in ARS 210.0.
  • ARS 210.0 "should be read in conjunction with Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity" (ARS 210.0, objective, verified). Terms in the forms are defined in clause 21 of ARS 210.0, many by pointing back to APS 210 (for example "HQLA1 has the meaning given in paragraph 9 of Attachment A of APS 210").
  • The data you report must be consistent with what the bank publishes (see section 8) and with what the bank has told APRA elsewhere.

For the guide's high-level view, see (Guide Ch 2, "Reporting to APRA and public disclosure").

An ADI that is neither an LCR ADI nor an MLH ADI reports only if APRA gives written notice (clause 7). Clause 8 sets out forms by class:

3.3 Levels: Level 1, Level 2 and domestic books (verified)

  • Level 1 is the ADI as one legal entity. Level 2 is the consolidated group (APS 210 para 3).
  • "If an ADI … is part of a Level 2 group, the ADI must also provide … information … designated for an ADI at Level 2 … unless the ADI is a subsidiary of an authorised NOHC", in which case the parent NOHC reports at Level 2 and "must comply with this Reporting Standard (other than [clause] 5) as if it were the relevant ADI" (ARS 210.0 clause 6).
  • Domestic books means the Australian operations of a foreign ADI's licensed branch.
  • Securitisation vehicles: where the ADI is the originating ADI in a securitisation, the vehicle's assets and liabilities are included in the LCR and the maturity forms regardless of capital relief, but excluded from the NSFR if the operational requirements for capital relief are met (ARF instructions; compare APS 210 para 5).

In the standard: The ARF 210.1A and 210.1B instructions say locally incorporated ADIs complete both at Level 1 and Level 2, and add that "for ADIs without a Level 2 consolidation ARF 210.1B is to be completed at Level 1" (verified in the form instructions).

Example Bank: it has one legal entity and no subsidiaries, so Level 1 equals Level 2. Whether a single-entity ADI lodges separate Level 1 and Level 2 returns, or only Level 1, is a point to confirm with APRA and your controller; the clause-6 duty is expressed for an ADI that is "part of a Level 2 group". (Unverified for a single-entity ADI.)

Other rules about timing:

3.6 The daily liquidity report, ARF 210.5

ARF 210.5 is not filed routinely under the text I verified. It is a form that APRA can call for in a time of concern. The ADI must then provide it daily "for the number of consecutive business days, or other duration or reporting frequency, specified by APRA at the time of the request" (ARS 210.0 clause 12). The information "must be given to APRA in an electronic form as notified by APRA at the time of APRA's request" (clause 10).

What it collects (form items, verified): unencumbered liquid assets (item 1); net funding outflows over the last five business days by retail, wholesale and financial institution (item 2); net contingent and committed outflows (item 3); net collateral and margin outflows (item 4); the LCR (or MLH ratio) over five business days (item 5); funding maturities from at call to six months (item 6); other contractual cash flows (item 7); and withdrawal notifications and buyback requests (item 8).

Watch out (unverified): In July 2026 APRA's notice on the end of the alternate submission process refers to a "Liquidity 210.5 monthly return" with a first mandatory APRA Connect period of 31 July 2026, and its migration support page lists "ADI Daily Liquidity (ARF 210.5)" with reporting beginning 31 May 2026. The verified text of ARS 210.0 says ARF 210.5 is submitted on APRA's request. I could not reconcile these. It may be that APRA has asked ADIs to report ARF 210.5 monthly under its power to vary reporting periods (clause 13), but I did not find that notice. Ask your controller whether Example Bank has a standing request from APRA, and keep the daily data ready either way (see document 10, section 7 for crisis data packs).

3.7 How returns are submitted

What the reporting standard says (verified).

  • Subject to the ARF 210.5 rule, the information "must be given to APRA in electronic format using an electronic method available on APRA's website or by a method notified by APRA prior to submission" (clause 9).
  • "When an officer or agent of an ADI submits information under this Reporting Standard using a method notified by APRA, the officer or agent must digitally sign the relevant information using a digital certificate acceptable to APRA" (clause 17).

What APRA's pages say about the system (verified, with gaps).

  • APRA Connect is "a data collection tool used to submit financial, statistical, and prudential information to APRA". ADI collections accept XML or XLSX uploads, not CSV. Uploaded data must match the reporting template, and upload history shows any failure reason.
  • Each reporting entity (identified by its Australian Business Number) must nominate one Regulatory Reporting Administrator (RRA). The RRA assigns roles, such as Preparer and Service Provider, to others. Users sign in through myGovID and the Relationship Authorisation Manager (RAM). Validation checks cover length, allowed characters, mandatory fields and permitted combinations. A successful submission triggers an email confirmation. An optional approval step (a "no auto submit" rule) can be requested from APRA.
  • APRA's notice dated 27 July 2026 says the alternate submission process ends for certain collections on set dates: "Liquidity 210.5 monthly return" from 31 July 2026, EFS from 31 August 2026, and "all other" migrated ADI collections from 30 September 2026. A footnote says the "all other" group "excludes forms for" several returns, including "ARS 210 - Liquidity".
  • APRA's migration page lists "ADI – Liquidity" with reporting beginning 30 September 2026 and does not itemise ARS 210 forms.
  • For help: APRA publishes contact details for submission concerns and for technical issues on the notice. Take them from APRA's site, not from this page.

Unverified: I could not confirm from APRA's pages exactly how the quarterly forms ARF 210.1A to 210.6 reach APRA today. The pages above point to APRA Connect, with the older "D2A" (data to APRA, with a digital certificate) route being phased out, but the notice's footnote leaves ARS 210 unclear. Ask your controller which route Example Bank used for the last quarter, who the RRA is, and who holds each role. Do not describe a submission system in a procedure, in writing, until this is confirmed against the last submission confirmation.

3.8 Quality control and authorisation (verified)

ARS 210.0 sets two quality duties.

  1. External auditor. "All information provided … must be the product of systems, processes and controls that have been reviewed and tested by the external auditor of the ADI as set out in Prudential Standard APS 310 Audit and Related Matters", and "this review and testing must be done on an annual basis or more frequently if required by the external auditor" (clause 15).
  2. Internal review and authorisation. All information "must be subject to systems, processes and controls developed by the ADI for the internal review and authorisation of that information. These systems, processes and controls are to assure the completeness and reliability of the information provided" (clause 16).

In practice you will meet the auditor each year asking to walk through the controls (data reconciliations, four-eyes review, sign-off). See document 12 (Controls, incidents and escalation).

3.9 Changes APRA can make

  • APRA may make minor variations to a form to correct "technical, programming or logical errors, inconsistencies or anomalies", or to clarify instructions, without changing any substantive requirement. If it does, it "will notify, in writing, each ADI that is required to report" (clauses 18 and 19).
  • A new version of the standard has a transition rule: an ADI reports under the old standard for any period that ended before the old standard was revoked (clause 20).

Controller's tip: When APRA notifies a change, log the date, the change, the owner and the first return affected. Then update the form template, the mapping (section 5) and the validation rules (section 6.5).

Three things to notice.

  1. The 210.1 forms carry three columns: (1) the market value or amount before any rate; (2) the weight (haircut, run-off rate or inflow rate), pre-set in the form "in accordance with the requirements of Attachment A of APS 210"; (3) the weighted amount, calculated by the form. So you report balances; the form applies the rates. A rate mismatch between your daily model and the form's pre-set weights is a red flag worth investigating.
  2. The maturity forms are contractual. "ADIs should not apply behavioural assumptions" to assets or liabilities, and must "not include new business volumes" (ARF 210.3.1 and 210.3.2 instructions). That is the contractual ladder of document 10, section 2.

6. The return production procedure

6.1 The shape of the procedure

Trigger: Each quarter end (30 September, 31 December, 31 March, 30 June for a 30 June financial year); annually for ARF 210.4 at the financial year end; and on request for ARF 210.5.

Inputs:

  1. The signed daily LCR for the quarter-end date, and the full series of signed daily LCRs for the quarter (document 08).
  2. The signed NSFR for the quarter-end month (document 09).
  3. The general ledger balance sheet at the quarter end, by product, counterparty type and residual maturity (document 07).
  4. The contractual cash flow data for the maturity forms (document 10, section 2).
  5. For ARF 210.4: the Board-approved funding strategy and forecast balance sheet (document 10, section 6).
  6. The current versions of the forms and instructions, and the mapping table (section 5).
  7. Last quarter's submitted return and its confirmation.

Outputs: The completed forms, the validation report, the reconciliation pack, the signed attestation, the submission confirmation, and a filed evidence pack.

Who prepares, reviews, signs: One analyst prepares, a second reviews, the controller signs (four-eyes rule, Example Bank convention). The senior officer who authorises the submission is set by the bank's own procedure (typical practice; confirm with your controller). ARS 210.0 requires "internal review and authorisation" controls (clause 16).

6.3 Step-by-step

  1. Confirm the forms. Check APRA's site for any new version of ARS 210.0 or notified minor variation (ARS 210.0 clauses 18 and 19). Record the version used.
  2. Freeze the data. Agree the cut-off with Finance. Record the extract IDs, times and the ledger close. After the freeze, no change without a logged exception approved by the controller (see 6.8).
  3. Prepare the LCR forms (ARF 210.1A and 210.1B). Take the balances (not the weighted amounts) from the signed daily LCR for the quarter-end date, map each classified line to its form item (section 5), and enter amounts in A$ millions to one decimal place. Let the form calculate the weights and totals. Enter zero where there is nothing to report.
  4. Calculate the period statistics. From the daily series, take the lowest, highest and mean end-of-day LCR over the quarter for items 32 to 34, using business days only. Item 31 is the Board-approved minimum LCR in the Liquidity Management Strategy (Example Bank: 120% in the Board risk appetite; confirm the figure in the strategy document).
  5. Prepare the NSFR form (ARF 210.6). Take the signed NSFR for the quarter-end month, fill the three residual-maturity columns, and compare the result with the form's calculation.
  6. Prepare the maturity forms (ARF 210.3.1 and 210.3.2). Extract contractual maturity data by product. Do not apply behavioural assumptions. Do not include new business. Report FX transaction legs gross in items 8 (inflows) and 7 (outflows) and do not net.
  7. Prepare ARF 210.4 (annually). Enter the actual year-end balances and the forecast balances at 3, 6, 12, 24 and 36 months from the Board-approved strategy.
  8. Run validation (section 6.5).
  9. Reconcile (section 6.6).
  10. Analytical review (section 6.7).
  11. Review, sign, attest (section 6.9).
  12. Submit and confirm (section 6.10).
  13. File the evidence (section 6.11).

6.4 The period statistics: a worked example

Suppose the signed daily LCR for five days of a short period were 147.2%, 146.0%, 148.5%, 149.9% and 145.1% (invented numbers; a real quarter has around 60 business days).

Lowest  = 145.1%
Highest = 149.9%
Mean    = (147.2 + 146.0 + 148.5 + 149.9 + 145.1) / 5 = 736.7 / 5 = 147.34, reported as 147.3%

The instruction asks for "end of day" LCRs and the "mean" using "business days only" (ARF 210.1A instructions). If a day is missing from the series, the mean is wrong, so the completeness of the series is a control (section 6.5).

6.6 Reconciliations

You prove the return in three directions.

To the daily LCR. The balances you enter must agree to the signed daily LCR for the quarter-end date. For Example Bank at the base position: HQLA 17.0, outflows 13.45, inflows 1.90, net 11.55, LCR 147.2%. In the form: item 4 = 17,000.0; item 27 = 13,450.0; item 28 = 1,900.0 (the cap of 10,087.5 is not binding; 75% of 13,450.0 = 10,087.5); item 29 = 13,450.0 - 1,900.0 = 11,550.0; item 30 = 17,000.0 / 11,550.0 = 147.2%. Any difference is explained in writing and corrected at source.

To the general ledger. Balance sheet totals on the maturity forms agree to the ledger at the quarter end (document 07, section on reconciliation to the ledger). Total assets and total liabilities and equity both equal A$100.0bn at the base position, so 100,000.0 in A$ millions. Differences caused by scope (for example, items APRA says to report gross, or FX legs) are documented.

To last quarter's return. Every line has a movement figure and a reason (section 6.7).

6.7 Analytical review against last period

Compare each key line with the previous quarter and with the same quarter last year. A simple rule (typical practice; confirm with your controller): investigate and comment on any line that moves by more than 10% and more than A$100m, and any change of sign.

Worked example. Suppose HQLA moved from A$17.0bn to A$15.0bn between two quarters. The change is -2.0 (-11.8%, since 2.0 / 17.0 = 11.76%). Both thresholds are exceeded, so the review notes must say why: for example, A$2.0bn of government bonds became encumbered under a repo (Att A para 23) and left the HQLA stock. A second reviewer checks that the explanation agrees with the collateral report and the daily LCR movements.

Also compare the ratio with the Board appetite and the minimum: an LCR that falls below 120% at the quarter end is already known from the daily process (document 12), and must not first appear in the return.

6.8 Data freeze and changes after it

A freeze is a line in time: after it, the data does not change without a reason and an approval. If you find an error after the freeze:

  1. Stop. Tell your controller the same day.
  2. Record what, where, how big and when found.
  3. Fix at source (the ledger, the classification or the mapping), re-extract, re-run the affected forms and all validation and reconciliation.
  4. Record the approval of the controller for the change.
  5. If the daily LCR for any day was affected, follow the incident rules in document 12.

Watch out: Never edit a number on the form to make a check pass. The form must be reproducible from the extract. If you cannot recreate the submitted number from the stored data, the return is not supported.

6.9 Review, sign-off and attestation

Review checklist (second analyst).

  • Right form version and reporting date.
  • All validation rules passed or each exception explained.
  • Reconciliations to the daily LCR, NSFR and ledger complete and signed.
  • Analytical commentary present and plausible.
  • Classification changes since last quarter listed, with approvals.
  • Mapping unchanged or change logged.
  • Evidence pack complete.

Controller review. Reads the commentary and the reconciliations, challenges two or three material lines, signs.

Attestation. ARS 210.0 does not set a form of words, but it requires internal authorisation controls (clause 16), and, when using an APRA-notified method, a digital signature by an officer or agent (clause 17). Example Bank's typical practice (confirm with your controller): the controller signs a preparation certificate; a more senior authorised officer approves the submission; Liquidity Risk gets a copy.

6.10 Submission and confirmation

See section 3.7 for the verified and unverified facts about the system. The procedure steps (typical practice) are:

  1. The submitter, a user with the right role, uploads the file (XML or XLSX if APRA Connect is used), after the final review.
  2. Check the upload result: every form accepted, with no failure reason.
  3. Wait for the confirmation message. APRA says a successful submission triggers an email confirmation (verified).
  4. Save the confirmation (with its time) into the evidence pack, and compare the submitted figures with the approved ones.
  5. Tell the controller, Treasury and Liquidity Risk that the return is in, with the key ratios.

If anything prevents submission by the due date (system outage, access problem), contact APRA immediately using the contact details on its notice (section 3.7) and tell your controller. Only APRA can extend the date (ARS 210.0 clause 14).

6.11 Record-keeping

Keep for each return: the extracts with data IDs; the mapping; the form files as submitted; the validation report; reconciliations; analytical review notes; review and sign-off records; the confirmation; correspondence with APRA. How long to keep them is set by the bank's records policy (typical practice: not less than seven years; confirm with your controller). The external auditor tests these controls each year (ARS 210.0 clause 15).

6.12 Resubmission when an error is found

Unverified: APRA's current guidance on resubmission was not checked. The steps below are typical practice; find the current method and contact details on APRA's website, or ask your controller, before the first time you do it.

Trigger: an error is found in a submitted return, by us, by the auditor or by APRA.

  1. Tell your controller immediately. Record the time found.
  2. Assess the size and effect: which forms and items, how much, whether any ratio changes, whether any limit or regulatory minimum is affected.
  3. If a regulatory minimum is affected (LCR or NSFR below 100% on the corrected numbers), treat it as an incident (document 12) and see the notification rules in section 7: inform APRA immediately (Att A para 5).
  4. Correct at source, re-run validation and reconciliation.
  5. Review and sign off the corrected return as for a new one.
  6. Contact APRA, explain the error and the correction, and resubmit through the method APRA specifies.
  7. Record the cause, the control that failed, and the fix.

Controls: the corrected figures reconcile; the error is added to the error log; a lessons-learned review within a set time. Evidence kept: the original, the corrected return, the explanation sent to APRA, the confirmation.

8. Public disclosure of the LCR and NSFR under APS 330

8.1 What public disclosure is, in everyday words

A return goes to APRA, privately. Public disclosure is what the bank publishes for everyone: investors, analysts, customers and other banks. A listed company publishes accounts so that outsiders can judge it. Banks also publish their capital and liquidity numbers. The idea is market discipline: if the world can see a bank's numbers, the bank has a reason to keep them sound. APS 330 Public Disclosure says its key principle is "clear, comprehensive, meaningful, consistent, and comparable public disclosures of prudential information … so as to contribute to the transparency of financial markets and to enhance market discipline" (APS 330 clause 11, verified).

The bank's Pillar 3 report is the common name for this publication (Pillar 3 is the Basel term for market disclosure; see Guide Ch 2 and Guide Ch 3).

8.3 What the standard requires for liquidity (verified, 2025 version)

  1. Follow the Basel templates. An ADI "must make the prudential disclosures as set out in the Standard made by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS Standard) titled 'Disclosure requirements', as the BCBS Standard exists from time to time, subject to the modifications specified in Attachment A" of APS 330 (clause 19). The BCBS standard covers, among others, "leverage ratio and liquidity" (clause 21).
  2. Frequency. Disclosures are published "as indicated in the relevant BCBS disclosure templates and tables" and vary "between quarterly, semi-annual and annual" (clause 31). They must be published "concurrently with the lodgement of the ADI's financial reports under the Corporations Act" (clause 32).
  3. Liquidity specifics (clauses 41 to 43). The ADI must disclose total HQLA and alternative liquid assets, and total liquid assets that are RBNZ eligible securities (clause 41). For the NSFR it is not required to disclose performing loans and securities with a risk weight of 35% or less under the Basel II standardised approach; instead it discloses standard residential property loans to individuals with an LVR of 80% or below, excluding encumbered loans and loans under one year (clause 42). Disclosures about "small business customers" use the SME definition in APS 210 (clause 43).
  4. MLH exemption. SFIs that use the minimum liquidity holdings approach are not required to make LCR or NSFR disclosures under the BCBS Standard (clause 44). Example Bank is an LCR ADI, so it is covered.
  5. Governance. - A Board-approved disclosure policy, with key elements summarised in the year-end public disclosure, setting out the approach to content and the internal controls, review and verification (clause 25). - "At a minimum, the same level of internal controls and procedures as for the ADI's financial reporting" (clause 26). - Disclosures must be "appropriately verified", and "consistent with information that has been subject to review by an external auditor, is lodged or published elsewhere or has been already supplied to APRA" (clauses 28 and 29). - An Accountable Person must attest in writing that the disclosures follow the disclosure policy, and the attestation is included in the disclosure (clause 30).
  6. Presentation. A "Regulatory Disclosures" section on the ADI's website; a standalone document; quantitative information also in CSV or Excel; and an archive of prior disclosures for at least the past 12 months (clauses 34 to 36). The liquidity information is included in the financial report, the directors' report, accompanying information or the website section with a link (clause 37).
  7. Modifications. Minor modifications to BCBS content are allowed where it conflicts with prudential standards, with a description and notice to APRA in advance (clause 20).
  8. APRA powers. APRA may require the ADI to rectify disclosures or to disclose further information (clause 23), or to commission an independent audit (clause 24).

8.5 The team's part

Finance (financial reporting) usually owns the Pillar 3 document. Liquidity Reporting provides the liquidity numbers and their support.

Procedure: producing the LIQ1 and LIQ2 inputs.

Trigger: the Pillar 3 calendar (each quarter, with the financial report date); the Finance team's request. Inputs: the signed daily LCR series for the quarter (HQLA, outflows by category, inflows, net outflows, LCR); the signed quarter-end NSFR; the BCBS template instructions and APRA modifications; the prior disclosure. Steps: (1) Confirm the template version and any APRA modifications. (2) Pull the daily values after haircuts, rates and caps. (3) Compute the simple averages over the quarter, and count the data points. (4) Map the categories to the template rows. (5) Prepare the NSFR quarter-end data. (6) Reconcile to the APRA return for the same period (the quarter-end figure and the mean must be consistent with item 34 of ARF 210.1A). (7) Write the commentary on drivers of change. (8) Review by a second analyst; controller sign-off; hand to Finance. Outputs: populated templates, commentary and reconciliation. Who reviews: second analyst; controller; Finance; the Accountable Person attests. Evidence kept: the daily series, calculations, reconciliations, the approved disclosure, the attestation.

Example (simple average). Suppose the daily HQLA were A$17.0bn, 16.8, 17.2 and 17.0 over four days (a real quarter has about 60). The simple average = (17.0 + 16.8 + 17.2 + 17.0) / 4 = 68.0 / 4 = A$17.0bn.

Watch out: A published figure is permanent and public. If you find an error in published disclosures, tell your controller at once; the Accountable Person and Finance decide how to correct it, and APRA may require changes (APS 330 clause 23).

9.2 Principles

  1. One voice. Requests go to a named coordinator (typically the Chief Financial Officer's office or Regulatory Affairs, not the analyst). Tell your controller the moment you receive a request or a query from anyone at APRA.
  2. Understand it. What exactly is asked, as at what date, at what level, and by when?
  3. Log it. Record the time received, from whom, the wording, the deadline, the owner.
  4. Accurate first, fast second. A wrong answer is worse than a short delay. If you need more time, the coordinator asks APRA.
  5. Consistent. The answer must agree with the returns, the daily LCR, the Board papers and the public disclosures.
  6. Evidence. Every number can be traced to a stored extract.
  7. Approved before it goes. The authorised officer approves the response.
  8. No new facts invented. If you do not know, say so and find out.
  9. Keep a copy of what was sent, and the confirmation of sending.

9.3 Procedure

Trigger: any request from APRA, or an auditor, for liquidity information. Inputs: the request; the log; the underlying data. Steps: (1) Tell the controller immediately. (2) Open a log entry. (3) Confirm the scope and deadline with the coordinator. (4) Gather data from the signed outputs, not from fresh extracts, unless the request is for a new analysis. (5) Prepare the response with sources noted. (6) Reconcile to the returns and disclosures. (7) Second-person check. (8) Approval by the authorised officer. (9) Send through the channel APRA specifies. (10) Record the confirmation and file. Controls: response agrees to prior submissions or the difference is explained; a named person approved; the log shows completion before the deadline. Outputs: the response and the log. Who reviews: second analyst, controller, authorised officer. Evidence kept: the request, the working papers, the response, the confirmation.

9.4 Supervisory reviews: getting ready

  • Keep an up-to-date "evidence index" for each requirement area: the framework documents, limits, the early warning set, stress test files, the funding strategy, the CFP, the data dictionary, classification rules, the control matrix, the return packs and the minutes of ALCO and the Board.
  • Keep a list of open issues and action owners.
  • Rehearse: the liquid asset operational test (Att A paras 22 to 23) and the contingency plan data pack (document 10, section 7) should be demonstrable in minutes.
  • On the day, answer the question asked, with evidence, and take actions away in writing.

Check yourself

  1. Example Bank's financial year ends on 30 June. By what date must the return for the quarter ending 31 December be lodged, and the funding plan ARF 210.4 for the year ending 30 June? Answer: Quarterly returns are due within 35 calendar days of quarter end (ARS 210.0 clause 11): 31 December plus 35 days is 4 February (31 days to 31 January, then 4 more days). ARF 210.4 is due within 35 calendar days of the end of the financial year: 30 June plus 35 days is 4 August.

  2. APRA asks for ARF 210.5 at 9:15 am on a Wednesday, and at 2:30 pm on a Thursday. What are the deadlines, and what date's data is used? Answer: The 9:15 am request (before 12 pm) must be answered by close of business the same Wednesday, using data at close of business on Tuesday. The 2:30 pm request (after 12 pm) must be answered by close of business on the next business day (Friday), using data at close of business on Thursday, the day of the request (ARS 210.0 clause 12).

  3. The signed end-of-day LCRs for four business days in a short period are 130.0%, 128.0%, 135.0% and 127.0%. What do items 32, 33 and 34 show? Answer: Lowest (item 32) 127.0%; highest (item 33) 135.0%; mean (item 34) = (130.0 + 128.0 + 135.0 + 127.0) / 4 = 520.0 / 4 = 130.0%.

  4. A bank reports total weighted outflows of A$13,450.0m and total inflows of A$20,000.0m. What goes in item 28, and what are the net cash outflows in item 29 (overlay zero)? Answer: The cap is 75% x 13,450.0 = 10,087.5. Inflows of 20,000.0 exceed it, so item 28 = 10,087.5. Item 29 = 13,450.0 - 10,087.5 = 3,362.5 (Att A para 30).

  5. Which forms does Example Bank not file, and why? Answer: ARF 210.2 (Minimum Liquidity Holdings), which is for MLH ADIs only. Example Bank is an LCR ADI. ARF 210.5 is filed only when APRA requests it (ARS 210.0 clauses 8 and 12).

  6. Which paragraph requires the bank to inform APRA immediately that the LCR has fallen below 100%, and what other provision is triggered when the stress is severe? Answer: (Att A para 5) for an actual or possible breach; (APS 210 para 14) for severe liquidity stress, which also requires advising the action being taken.

  7. Between two quarters, HQLA moves from A$17.0bn to A$15.8bn. Using the rule "investigate lines that move by more than 10% and more than A$100m", is an investigation required? Answer: The change is -1.2bn (A$1,200m), which is more than A$100m. As a percentage, 1.2 / 17.0 = 7.1%, which is below 10%. The rule needs both conditions, so it is not required by the rule. The reviewer may still ask for a comment because the change is more than A$100m.

  8. What are the two quality duties in ARS 210.0 clauses 15 and 16? Answer: Information must come from systems, processes and controls reviewed and tested by the external auditor (under APS 310) at least annually; and it must be subject to the ADI's own internal review and authorisation controls to assure completeness and reliability.

  9. What is the difference between the quarter-end LCR in the APRA return and the LCR in the public disclosure template LIQ1? Answer: The return reports the position at the quarter-end date (with the lowest, highest and mean over the period as separate items); LIQ1 reports averages of daily observations over the quarter. They are different measures, so a reconciliation memo explains the difference (BIS DIS85; APS 330 clause 19).

  10. When must a bank approach APRA before approaching the RBA, and what must it be able to provide? Answer: Before requesting Exceptional Liquidity Assistance, the bank must notify APRA immediately of its liquidity concerns and its intention to request ELA, and must be operationally able to provide information with the application or within the timeframes APRA sets (APS 210 para 70).

What to remember

  • A return is the same numbers as the daily LCR and the monthly NSFR, laid out in APRA's boxes, signed and sent by a legal due date.
  • Prudential standards (APS 210) set the rules; reporting standards (ARS 210.0) set the forms, timing and method; APS 330 sets public disclosure.
  • Example Bank files ARF 210.1A, 210.1B, 210.3.1, 210.3.2, 210.4 and 210.6, and ARF 210.5 on request. Quarterly forms and the annual plan are due within 35 calendar days; ARF 210.5 within a day.
  • The maturity forms are contractual only, with no behavioural assumptions and no new business.
  • Items 32 to 34 of the LCR form need a complete daily series: one missing day makes the statistics wrong.
  • Freeze, validate, reconcile to the daily LCR and ledger, review, sign, submit, keep the confirmation. Never edit a number to make a check pass.
  • The notifications table is your emergency list: (APS 210 para 14), (Att A para 5) and (APS 210 para 70) are immediate; others are on request or annual.
  • APS 330 requires the BCBS templates, a Board-approved disclosure policy, an Accountable Person attestation and consistency with what APRA has been given.
  • Confirm the unverified items (submission route, ARF 210.5 monthly instruction, SFI status, resubmission method) with your controller and APRA before relying on them.
