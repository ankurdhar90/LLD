08 Producing the daily LCR, end to end

Purpose: To give you a complete operating procedure for the daily liquidity coverage ratio (LCR), from the data at close of business to the signed and distributed result, with the Example Bank figures worked line by line so that you can check your own work against them.

You will be able to:

  • Run the daily LCR in the right order, with the checks that belong at each step.
  • Value the liquid asset portfolio, confirm eligibility, apply haircuts and caps, and weight outflows and inflows with the correct paragraph for each line.
  • Reproduce the Example Bank LCR of 147.2% and explain each line.
  • Explain a change in the ratio line by line, and project the ratio forward to spot debt rolling into the 30-day window.
  • Know what to do when the ratio is below a trigger, below Board appetite, or below 100%.
  • Review and sign off the LCR using the controls and checklist.

Before you start: Read document 02 (Liquidity from zero) and document 07 (Data and classification). Keep document 05 (companion to Attachment A) open as you work. You will use the paragraph references in this document to find the matching entries.

Time: About six hours: two to read, two to rebuild the Example Bank working paper by hand, and two for the worked examples and Check yourself questions.

1. What the daily LCR is, in everyday words

The LCR asks one question: if the bank were hit by a severe shock for 30 days, would it have enough easy-to-sell assets to pay everything that is likely to leave? The answer is a ratio.

LCR = Stock of HQLA / Total net cash outflows over the next 30 calendar days

(Att A para 3). HQLA means high-quality liquid assets: assets the bank can turn into cash quickly and reliably. The bank must hold unencumbered HQLA (assets that are not pledged to anyone) to meet its liquidity needs for a 30 calendar day period under a severe stress scenario (APS 210 para 55; Att A para 1).

The LCR has two parts (Att A para 2):

  • the numerator: the value of the stock of HQLA; and
  • the denominator: total net cash outflows, calculated using the scenario rates in Attachment A.

Net cash outflows are expected outflows minus expected inflows, with the inflows capped at 75% of outflows (Att A para 30):

Total net cash outflows = Total expected cash outflows
                          - Min { total expected cash inflows ; 75% x total expected cash outflows }

1.1 The minimum and why we compute it daily

A locally incorporated ADI (authorised deposit-taking institution) such as Example Bank must keep both its Australian-dollar (AUD) LCR and its all-currencies LCR at or above 100% (APS 210 para 55(a)). The bank must meet the minimum on a continuous basis, absent a situation of financial stress (APS 210 para 54). "Continuous" means every day, not just at month-end. So Example Bank produces the LCR daily.

The standard also says the ADI must include an appropriate buffer of HQLA over the LCR requirement in line with its liquidity risk tolerance (Att A para 4). Example Bank's buffer is expressed in two internal levels (section 2.3).

1.2 Example Bank simplifications

Example Bank has one legal entity and one currency. That means:

  • Level 1 equals Level 2. Level 1 is the ADI on its own; Level 2 is the consolidated banking group (APS 210 para 3). With one entity there is one calculation, reported on both bases.
  • The AUD LCR equals the all-currencies LCR. All assets and flows are in Australian dollars (APS 210 para 55(a)).
  • There is no Committed Liquidity Facility (CLF). The CLF was a standby arrangement with the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) that could count towards the numerator (Att A paras 13 to 21). The aggregate CLF was phased out to zero as at 1 January 2023 (APRA), so no CLF line appears in the numerator.

From the controller: Even though the two ratios are identical, I want you to compute both every day, as separate columns. If they ever differ for Example Bank, you have a currency flag error in the data. That makes it a free control.

5. The procedure, step by step

Step 1: Confirm all feeds have arrived

  1. Open the feed arrival checklist. Tick each of the feeds in section 3 as it lands.
  2. For each feed, compare the record count and the total balance with the control totals in the feed footer.
  3. If a feed is late, phone the data owner. Note the time. If still missing at 08:00, tell the controller.

Control: No calculation starts until all feeds pass the completeness check, or the controller approves an exception. Output: A completed feed checklist.

Step 2: Load and reconcile the data

  1. Load the feeds into staging.
  2. Reconcile the loaded totals to the general ledger by ledger line. Total assets must equal total liabilities and equity, and each must agree to the trial balance (document 07 section 9).
  3. Investigate any difference. A small timing difference with a known cause may be accepted by the controller; an unexplained difference is not.

Control: Zero unexplained difference between the feeds and the general ledger. Output: A reconciliation report, kept as evidence.

Step 3: Run the data quality checks and classification

  1. Run the validity, consistency and duplicate checks (document 07 section 9).
  2. Run the classification rules (document 07 sections 6 and 7). Every record gets an LCR category, rate and paragraph.
  3. Clear the exceptions. Where a field is missing, take the conservative category and log it (document 07 section 10).

Control: The exceptions log is reviewed by the preparer and the reviewer. Defaults are reported in the pack. Output: A classified dataset and an exceptions log.

Step 4: Value the liquid asset portfolio at market value

  1. List the securities in Treasury's liquid asset portfolio with their closing market prices.
  2. Multiply units by price to get market value. HQLA1 is included at market value (Att A para 9); HQLA2 is included at current market value after the haircut (Att A paras 10 and 12).
  3. Compare prices with the independent price source. Escalate large differences to Treasury and Market Risk.
  4. Confirm the Exchange Settlement Account balance with the RBA statement. Notes and coin are taken from the cash balance report.

Watch out: The NSFR uses accounting carrying value. The LCR numerator uses market value. If an Australian Government bond is carried at A$100.3m but trades at A$98.5m, the LCR counts A$98.5m (document 07 Example 6).

In the standard: An ADI with minimum liquidity holdings (MLH) status must value liquid assets at market value under the July 2025 version of the standard. Example Bank is an LCR ADI, so Attachment A already requires market value for the stock of HQLA. See document 10 for the MLH regime in brief.

Control: Prices tie to the independent source; ESA balance ties to the RBA statement. Output: The valued portfolio.

Step 5: Confirm HQLA eligibility and the operational requirements

For each asset, confirm that it belongs in the stock of HQLA.

  1. Eligible class. Is it in HQLA1 (Att A para 9), HQLA2A (Att A para 10) or HQLA2B (Att A para 12)? For Example Bank, only notes and coin, RBA balances, and Australian Government and semi-government securities are HQLA, all HQLA1; no AUD assets currently qualify as HQLA2 (per APRA's liquidity FAQ). Use the decision tree S in document 07 section 7.1.
  2. Held on the first day of the stress, whatever the residual maturity. Assets are in the stock if the bank is holding them on day one, irrespective of residual maturity (Att A para 6).
  3. Operational requirements (Att A paras 22 and 23). Treasury must confirm each of these for every asset in the stock: - the asset is available to be converted into cash at any time (Att A para 22(a)); - it is unencumbered and under the control of the function charged with managing liquidity risk, which typically is the treasurer, who must have continuous authority, and the legal and operational capability, to monetise it (Att A para 22(b)); - control is evidenced by holding it in a separate pool managed with the sole intent to use as a source of contingent funds, or by demonstrating that the function can monetise the asset at any point in the 30-day stress period and that the proceeds are available to the function without directly conflicting with a stated business or risk management strategy (Att A para 22(c)); and - it is not pledged to secure, collateralise or credit enhance any transaction, and not designated to cover operational costs such as rents and salaries (Att A para 23).
  4. Encumbrance. Compare the securities list with the collateral feed. Any security pledged (for example in a repo) is removed from the stock. Securities received in a reverse repo or securities financing transaction may be included only if held at the ADI, not rehypothecated, and legally and contractually available for the ADI's use (Att A para 23).
  5. Assets that have become ineligible. If an asset becomes ineligible (for example through a rating downgrade), the ADI may keep it in the stock for an additional 30 calendar days to adjust or replace it (Att A para 25). Record the date the asset became ineligible and the last day it may remain.
  6. Level 2. At Level 2, liquid assets held by a legal entity to meet its own requirements may be included only under Att A para 24. Example Bank has one entity, so this does not arise.
  7. No double counting. An asset in the stock must not also produce an inflow (Att A para 31).

Control: Treasury confirms each day, in writing, the list of eligible assets and that the operational requirements are met. The preparer compares it with the collateral feed. Output: The eligible stock list.

Step 6: Apply haircuts and caps

  1. Haircuts (a haircut reduces market value to allow for a fall in price in a stress): HQLA1 none (Att A para 9); HQLA2A 15% (Att A para 10); HQLA2B residential mortgage-backed securities 25% (Att A para 12(a)); other HQLA2B (corporate debt and common equity) 50% (Att A para 12(b) and (c)).
  2. Caps (Att A paras 6 to 8): - HQLA2 (A and B together) may be at most 40% of the total stock. - HQLA2B may be at most 15% of the total stock. - The caps are calculated on adjusted amounts, after unwinding secured funding, secured lending and collateral swap transactions maturing within 30 calendar days (Att A paras 7 and 8). - Maximum adjusted HQLA2 = two-thirds of adjusted HQLA1 after haircuts. - Maximum adjusted HQLA2B = the lower of 15/85 of the sum of adjusted HQLA1 and HQLA2A after haircuts, and (where the 40% cap is binding) one quarter of adjusted HQLA1 after haircuts (Att A para 7).
Max HQLA2  = 2/3  x  adjusted HQLA1
Max HQLA2B = lower of  [ 15/85 x (adjusted HQLA1 + adjusted HQLA2A) ]
                  and  [ 1/4 x adjusted HQLA1 ]
Counted HQLA = HQLA1 + min(HQLA2A + HQLA2B, Max HQLA2) with HQLA2B <= Max HQLA2B
  1. Example Bank: only HQLA1 is held, so no haircut or cap applies. HQLA = A$17.0bn.

Example (illustration only, not Example Bank, as no AUD HQLA2 currently qualifies): A bank has HQLA1 of A$60 and HQLA2A with market value A$100. After the 15% haircut, HQLA2A is A$85. The maximum HQLA2 is two-thirds of 60 = A$40. HQLA2A is cut to A$40. Counted HQLA = 60 + 40 = A$100. HQLA2 is 40% of the stock, exactly at the cap.

Example (illustration only): HQLA1 70, HQLA2A 20 and HQLA2B 20 (all after haircuts). The 15/85 limit is 15/85 x (70 + 20) = 15.88. The one-quarter limit is 70/4 = 17.5. The lower is 15.88, so HQLA2B counted is 15.88. Total HQLA2 is 20 + 15.88 = 35.88, below the 2/3 cap of 46.67. Counted HQLA = 70 + 20 + 15.88 = 105.88. HQLA2B is 15.88 / 105.88 = 15.0% of the stock.

Control: The reviewer recomputes the cap formulas independently. Where there is no HQLA2, the reviewer confirms that none has been included. Output: Counted HQLA, by class.

Step 7: Classify and weight the cash outflows

Use the decision trees in document 07 for each product. The outflow categories and rates are in Att A Table 3 (Att A para 53).

  1. Retail and qualifying SME deposits (Att A paras 34 to 40 and 46): stable 5%, less stable 10%, or 25% if the scorecard total is 3 or more.
  2. Term deposits over 30 days with no legal right to withdraw, or a materially penalising early withdrawal: excluded, 0% (Att A para 41). If the bank lets depositors out despite the clause, the entire category is demand (Att A para 42).
  3. Operational deposits (Att A paras 47 to 50): 5% if fully insured, 25% if not. Excess balances are non-operational.
  4. Non-operational deposits from non-financial corporations, sovereigns, central banks, PSEs, MDBs and SMEs above A$2m: 20% if fully insured, 40% if not.
  5. All other non-operational deposits (including financial institutions): 100%.
  6. Unsecured wholesale debt inside 30 days, including options exercisable by the investor inside 30 days: 100% (Att A para 45; Table 3).
  7. Secured funding maturing inside 30 days: by collateral (HQLA1 0%, HQLA2A 15%, and so on, Att A Table 3).
  8. Derivatives and collateral: net cash outflows by counterparty at 100%; downgrade collateral at 100%; the other collateral lines (Att A Table 3 and footnote 5).
  9. Committed facilities: undrawn portion by customer and purpose (Att A paras 51 and 52; Table 3).
  10. Other contingent funding: trade finance, guarantees, buybacks, and others (Att A Table 3).
  11. Other contractual cash outflows inside 30 days: 100% (Att A Table 3).

Multiply each balance by its rate. Sum.

From the controller: Not every liability produces an outflow. Anything due after the 30-day window produces nothing today, such as the A$20.0bn of Example Bank's wholesale debt that matures after 30 days. That is why we run the forward-looking LCR in section 9.

Control: Totals by category reconcile to the classified dataset; the reviewer recomputes the larger lines. Output: Weighted outflows by line.

Step 8: Classify and weight the cash inflows

  1. Include only contractual inflows from fully performing exposures for which no default is expected within 30 days (Att A para 54).
  2. Take each inflow at the latest possible date based on the counterparty's contractual rights. Loans without a specific maturity give only minimum payments (Att A para 60).
  3. Apply the rates: - retail and SME customers: 50% (Att A para 61); - financial institutions and central banks: 100% (Att A para 62(a)); - other entities (non-financial corporates, sovereigns, PSEs, MDBs): 50% (Att A para 62(b)); - maturing securities not in the stock of HQLA: 100% (Att A para 62); - maturing secured lending: by the collateral held (Att A para 55 and Table 4); - derivatives inflows: 100% (Att A para 65); - other contractual inflows: 100%; non-financial revenues are not taken into account (Att A para 66); - operational deposits held at other financial institutions: 0% (Att A para 63); deposits at the centralised institution of a cooperative network: 0% (Att A para 64); - lines of credit, liquidity facilities and other contingent funding facilities the ADI holds at other institutions: 0% (Att A para 57).
  4. Do not double count. If the asset is in the stock of HQLA, its inflow is not counted (Att A para 31).

Control: Inflow lines are tied to the loan feed's scheduled repayments. A performing flag must be present for every inflow. Output: Weighted inflows by line.

Step 9: Apply the 75% cap on inflows

Cap = 75% x total expected cash outflows
Inflows counted = Min { total expected inflows ; cap }
Net cash outflows = total outflows - inflows counted

(Att A para 30). The cap protects the bank from relying on inflows that might not arrive. On Example Bank's base position the cap is 75% x A$13.45bn = A$10.09bn (10.0875), against inflows of A$1.90bn, so the cap does not bite.

Watch out: APRA may set a lower limit on inflows if it thinks a bank relies too much on a few wholesale counterparties (Att A para 54). Keep the data to show inflows by counterparty.

Control: Test the cap every day, even when it is not binding. Record the cap value in the working paper. Output: Net cash outflows.

Step 10: Compute the AUD and all-currencies ratios

  1. Compute the all-currencies LCR = counted HQLA / net cash outflows.
  2. Compute the AUD LCR using only AUD HQLA and AUD flows. Example Bank has one currency, so both equal the same figure.
  3. Both must be at least 100% for a locally incorporated ADI (APS 210 para 55(a)). For a foreign ADI the minimum is 40% on an all-currencies basis (APS 210 para 55(b)), but Example Bank is locally incorporated.
  4. Compute the surplus = HQLA minus net cash outflows. This is the amount of liquid assets above the minimum.
  5. Check that the AUD and all-currencies results agree; a difference signals a currency flag problem.

Control: AUD LCR equals all-currencies LCR. The result is shown to one decimal place. Output: The LCR and the surplus.

Step 11: Compare with the limits

  1. Compare with the regulatory minimum (100%), the Board risk appetite (120%) and the management trigger (125%).
  2. Compute headroom to each (section 6.4).
  3. If any line is breached, go to section 12 straight away. Do not wait for the review.

Output: A limit status of green, amber, red (appetite breach) or black (regulatory minimum breach). Labels are an Example Bank convention.

Step 12: Analyse the day's movement

Compare today's LCR with yesterday's. Explain the change line by line (section 8). A change that you cannot explain is an unresolved error until proved otherwise.

Control: The explained movement must add up to the total movement. Output: A short written commentary.

Step 13: Preparer self-check and second-person review

  1. The preparer completes the checklist (section 13) and passes the working paper to the reviewer.
  2. The reviewer independently re-performs: the HQLA total, the three or four largest outflow lines, the inflow cap, the ratio, and the movement bridge. The reviewer also confirms the reconciliation was clean and that the exception log has been read.
  3. Differences are resolved before the controller sees the paper.

Control: The reviewer is not the preparer. Output: A reviewed working paper with reviewer sign-off.

Step 14: Controller review and sign-off

  1. The controller reads the commentary, challenges any unusual movement, and checks the limit status.
  2. The controller signs. If the controller is away, the delegate named in the delegations schedule signs (typical practice; confirm with your controller).

Output: A signed LCR.

Step 15: Distribute and file

  1. Send the daily LCR report to the distribution list (section 13.2) by 10:00 am.
  2. Save the evidence pack (section 13.3).
  3. Update the daily LCR log, which holds the history used for movement analysis and trend charts.
  4. Run the forward-looking projection (section 9) and send it to Treasury.

Output: The distributed report and the filed evidence.

7. A day in the life of the daily LCR

This narrative walks through one Tuesday at Example Bank. The numbers are the base position above, so you can follow with the working paper. The times are the Example Bank convention.

06:45. You arrive early to check the batch. The overnight batch has finished. Technology has posted its all-clear message.

07:15. You open the feed arrival checklist. Nine of the ten feeds are in. The collateral feed is missing.

07:30. You phone collateral management. The feed was delayed by 20 minutes by a file transfer fault. It lands at 07:50. You note the time in the log.

07:55. You load all feeds and reconcile to the general ledger. Total assets A$100.0bn and total liabilities and equity A$100.0bn both agree to the trial balance, with zero unexplained difference.

08:15. Treasury sends its confirmation: A$17.0bn of eligible HQLA1, all unencumbered, held in the liquid asset pool under the treasurer's control (Att A para 22). You compare with the collateral feed: no government securities are pledged. The Exchange Settlement Account balance agrees to the RBA statement.

08:30. The classification run finishes. There is one exception: a new corporate deposit of A$12m has no sector code. You default it to financial institution, non-operational at 100% (document 07 section 10) so the calculation can continue, and log it. You ring the relationship manager.

08:50. The relationship manager confirms the customer is a non-financial company and the account is not operational. You correct the code at source, reverse the default, and note that the A$12m takes 40%. 8. Movement analysis: explaining the change in the ratio

Every morning someone senior asks, "Why did it move?" Your commentary should answer before they ask. The method has six steps.

  1. Compute the total change in the LCR in percentage points, today minus yesterday.
  2. Split it into two effects using the bridge below: the effect of the numerator (HQLA) and the effect of the denominator (net cash outflows).
  3. Go line by line. For each HQLA line and each outflow and inflow line, compute today minus yesterday in A$bn.
  4. Add up. The line changes must sum to the change in HQLA and to the change in net outflows. If they do not, an error is hiding.
  5. Find the business cause for each material line by asking Treasury, the product owner or the data owner. "Balances moved" is not a cause. "A A$2.0bn fixed-term wholesale deposit was repaid" is.
  6. Write the commentary: the ratio, the change, the main drivers in order of size, whether any limit status changed, and any action.

8.1 The bridge

LCR  = H / N          (H = counted HQLA, N = net cash outflows)

Numerator effect   = (H1 - H0) / N0
Denominator effect = H1 x ( 1/N1 - 1/N0 )
Total change       = Numerator effect + Denominator effect

H0 and N0 are yesterday's figures; H1 and N1 today's. The two effects add exactly to the change in the ratio.

8.2 Worked example 1: A$2.0bn of financial institution deposits replaced by retail term deposits over 30 days

The event. A$2.0bn of at-call financial institution deposits leave. Treasury replaces them with A$2.0bn of retail term deposits with residual maturity over 30 days, no legal right to withdraw early and a materially penalising early withdrawal. Cash is neutral: money out and money in are equal, so HQLA is unchanged.

Commentary. "LCR rose 30.8 points to 178.0%. A$2.0bn of financial institution deposits (100% run-off) were replaced by retail term deposits over 30 days that qualify for exclusion (0% run-off, Att A para 41). HQLA is unchanged at A$17.0bn. Net outflows fell A$2.00bn to A$9.55bn."

Watch out: The 0% depends on the retail term deposits really being term. Test the legal right to withdraw and the penalty (Att A para 41) and the pool test (Att A para 42). If the new deposits had instead been treated as less stable demand deposits at 10%, outflows would be 11.65, net 9.75, and the LCR 17.0 / 9.75 = 174.4%. If they are 0% in the LCR but were in fact withdrawable, the ratio is overstated by 3.6 points.

From the controller: The NSFR moves too. Swapping at-call financial institution funding (0% ASF) for less stable retail term deposits (90% ASF) adds A$1.8bn of available stable funding: (79.75 + 1.80) / 58.50 = 139.4%. A funding change often helps both ratios. Document 09 shows how to explain it there.

8.3 Worked example 2: A$3.0bn of cash lent as mortgages

The event. Example Bank lends A$3.0bn out of its Exchange Settlement Account as new standard residential mortgages. The cash leaves the HQLA stock. From the controller: The NSFR goes the other way. Lending cash to customers uses up liquidity and also uses up stable funding. Treasury should have known this before approving the lending.

9. The forward-looking LCR

The LCR you compute each morning looks at the next 30 days from yesterday's close. It cannot see a large debt maturity five weeks out. But that maturity will sit inside the window soon, and the bank must have a reliable management information system that provides timely and forward-looking information on the liquidity position (APS 210 para 40). The bank must also have a robust framework for projecting cash flows arising from assets, liabilities and off-balance sheet items over an appropriate set of time horizons (APS 210 para 36). The forward-looking LCR delivers both.

9.1 Method

  1. Choose the horizon. Project the next 30 to 90 days, one projection date per business day (or per week for the outer weeks).
  2. For each projection date d, the window is the 30 calendar days from d+1 to d+30.
  3. Build the contractual maturity ladder from the daily data: deposits maturing, wholesale debt maturing, repos, derivatives, facilities and loan repayments, by date.
  4. For each date, add to the window the outflows that fall inside it and drop those that have passed. Apply the usual Att A rates.
  5. Update HQLA for known events: debt repaid reduces cash; issuance and planned asset purchases increase it. Add Treasury's funding plan.
  6. Compute the LCR for each projection date and compare with 125%, 120% and 100%.
  7. Report the dates on which a trigger would be crossed, so Treasury can act early.
  8. Refresh daily, or weekly in calm periods, and keep each projection so it can be compared with what actually happened.

State the assumptions on the report: for example "static balance sheet except for contractual maturities; no refinancing." A projection with no assumptions is unreadable.

9.2 Worked example: debt rolling into the window

Example Bank's base position (HQLA 17.0, outflows 13.45, inflows 1.90) with A$3.0bn of wholesale debt maturing between 30 days and 6 months: A$1.0bn on day 35, A$1.2bn on day 50 and A$0.8bn on day 75. Assume everything else is static, nothing is refinanced and each maturity is paid from the RBA balance.

Projection date Window Debt in the window HQLA Outflows Net outflows LCR Status
Day 0 1 to 30 none 17.0 13.45 11.55 147.2% Green
Day 5 6 to 35 1.0 (day 35) 17.0 14.45 12.55 135.5% Green
Day 20 21 to 50 1.0 and 1.2 17.0 15.65 13.75 123.6% Amber (below 125%)
Day 36 37 to 66 1.2 16.0 14.65 12.75 125.5% Green
Day 45 46 to 75 1.2 and 0.8 16.0 15.45 13.55 118.1% Below Board appetite
Day 51 52 to 81 0.8 14.8 14.25 12.35 119.8% Below Board appetite
Day 76 77 to 106 none 14.0 13.45 11.55 121.2% Amber

Workings: net outflows = outflows - 1.90 (the cap, 75% of outflows, is always above 1.90 here). Day 5: 17.0 / 12.55 = 1.3546. Day 20: 17.0 / 13.75 = 1.2364. Day 36: 16.0 / 12.75 = 1.2549. Day 45: 16.0 / 13.55 = 1.1808. Day 51: 14.8 / 12.35 = 1.1984. Day 76: 14.0 / 11.55 = 1.2121.

What the projection shows. The bank never falls below 100%, but it crosses the amber trigger around day 20 and Board appetite around day 45, purely because debt matures and is paid from liquid assets. Nothing is wrong today. The risk is the plan.

Action. Treasury issues A$2.0bn of debt with a term beyond 30 days before day 36 and holds the proceeds as RBA balances. HQLA at day 45 becomes 17.0 + 2.0 - 1.0 (the day-35 maturity paid) = 18.0, outflows still 15.45, net 13.55, LCR = 18.0 / 13.55 = 132.8%. At day 76 HQLA is 18.0 - 1.2 - 0.8 = 16.0, so LCR = 16.0 / 11.55 = 138.5%.

From the controller: Look again at the day-45 row. A forward projection run six weeks earlier would have shown the dip while there was plenty of time to arrange funding. The incident scenario in document 12 shows what happens when nobody did.

9.3 Spotting debt rolling in

Add these checks to the daily routine:

  • Sort the wholesale debt and term deposit ladder by maturity date, and flag any amount over a set size in the 31 to 90 day buckets.
  • For each flagged item, check whether Treasury's funding plan replaces it.
  • Check investor option dates. A put exercisable by the investor inside the window counts in full, even if the final maturity is years away (Att A para 45).
  • Watch debt that moves from over 30 days to under 30 days: the first day it is inside the window, the outflow arrives and the LCR drops overnight.
  • Compare each projection with the previous one, and say what changed.

10.3 Tolerance checks

Typical practice: The following tolerances are common; Example Bank's are set in its procedure. Confirm with your controller.

  • Ratio movement: explain any day-on-day change greater than 3 percentage points.
  • Line movement: explain any outflow line or HQLA line that moves by more than 5% or A$0.2bn, whichever is smaller.
  • Reasonableness: the LCR should stay between recent highs and lows; a number far outside history is guilty until proved innocent.
  • Mix: the share of deposits by category should not shift unless there is a known cause.

10.4 The four-eyes rule

The preparer cannot review. The reviewer cannot sign. The controller cannot be the preparer. Each person signs with date and time. A review that was not documented did not happen.

12. What to do when the LCR is below a limit

The three levels use Example Bank conventions except the regulatory minimum.

Level Condition Immediate steps
Amber LCR below 125% (management trigger) but at or above 120% Tell the controller before distribution. Distribute with the trigger flagged. Controller informs the head of Treasury and Liquidity Risk the same morning. Treasury states what it will do. Include in the next ALCO pack
Red LCR below 120% (Board appetite) but at or above 100% Controller informs the head of Treasury, Liquidity Risk, and the executive responsible for Finance immediately. Convene ALCO or its chair. Agree an action plan with a date. Tell the Board Risk Committee in line with the policy. A breach of a limit requires a plan of action to review the exposure and reduce it to within the limit (APS 210 para 37). The Board and senior management must be informed immediately of material and/or persistent breaches of limits and a significant decline in the cushion of unencumbered liquid assets (APS 210 para 33)
Black LCR below 100%, or circumstances that may result in a breach Controller informs the head of Treasury, Liquidity Risk and the most senior executives immediately. Inform APRA immediately (Att A para 5; APS 210 para 14). Consider the contingency funding plan (APS 210 paras 47 to 51). Follow document 12

The standard says an ADI "must inform APRA immediately in the event of an actual breach of its LCR requirement or if it becomes aware of circumstances that may result in a breach" (Att A para 5). A forward-looking projection that shows a future breach is exactly such a circumstance. APS 210 para 14 also says that if an ADI experiences a severe liquidity stress, it must notify APRA immediately and advise the action being taken. Notifications are made by the right people, not by the preparer; but you must make sure the right people know straight away. Document 11 lists the notifications; document 12 gives the incident procedure with times and messages.

Watch out: Never adjust a number to avoid a trigger. If you think a classification is wrong, say so and fix it with the proper change process (document 07 section 11).

Typical remedies Treasury considers: raising term funding beyond 30 days and holding the proceeds as RBA balances; buying HQLA; extending or replacing maturing debt; slowing lending; changing the deposit mix. These are Treasury's decisions, not yours.

Check yourself

  1. Example Bank has HQLA of A$17.0bn, outflows of A$13.45bn and inflows of A$1.90bn. Calculate the LCR, the inflow cap and the surplus.

Answer: Cap = 75% x 13.45 = 10.0875. Inflows counted = min(1.90 ; 10.0875) = 1.90. Net = 13.45 - 1.90 = 11.55. LCR = 17.0 / 11.55 = 147.2%. Surplus = 17.0 - 11.55 = 5.45.

  1. A bank has outflows of 100 and inflows of 90. What are net outflows?

Answer: Cap = 75 (75% x 100). Inflows counted = min(90 ; 75) = 75. Net = 100 - 75 = 25 (Att A para 30).

  1. Example Bank replaces A$1.0bn of at-call financial institution deposits with retail term deposits over 30 days that qualify for exclusion. What is the new LCR?

Answer: Outflows fall by 1.0 x 100% = 1.0, to 12.45. Net = 12.45 - 1.90 = 10.55. LCR = 17.0 / 10.55 = 161.1%. (Cap = 9.34, not binding.)

  1. A bank has HQLA1 of A$90 and HQLA2A with a market value of A$70. How much HQLA counts? What if the market value were A$100?

Answer: (a) After a 15% haircut, HQLA2A = 59.5. Cap = 2/3 x 90 = 60. 59.5 is below 60, so the total is 90 + 59.5 = 149.5. (b) At A$100: 85 after haircut, which exceeds 60, so only 60 counts: 90 + 60 = 150. (Att A paras 6, 7 and 10.)

  1. Example Bank's ESA balance falls by A$0.5bn to pay a supplier. Nothing else changes. What is the new LCR and the change?

Answer: HQLA = 16.5. LCR = 16.5 / 11.55 = 142.9%. Change = -0.5 / 11.55 = -4.3 points (147.2% to 142.9%). It is still above the 125% trigger. A 4.3-point move exceeds the usual tolerance, so explain it.

  1. By how much can Example Bank's HQLA fall, with nothing else changing, before it reaches the 125% amber trigger?

Answer: HQLA at 125% = 1.25 x 11.55 = 14.4375. The fall that can be absorbed = 17.0 - 14.4375 = A$2.56bn.

  1. A bond issue of A$1.0bn matures on day 35, and nothing else matures. On which projection date does it first enter the LCR window, and what is the projected LCR on that date? (Base position.)

Answer: The window on date d covers days d+1 to d+30. It includes day 35 first when d = 5. Outflows = 13.45 + 1.0 = 14.45. Net = 12.55. LCR = 17.0 / 12.55 = 135.5%.

  1. Why can a reverse repo bond in the HQLA stock not also produce an inflow when it matures?

Answer: The ADI must not double count (Att A para 31). If the asset is in the stock of HQLA, its associated cash inflows cannot also be counted. Also, reverse repos secured by HQLA1 are assumed to be rolled over and give 0% inflow (Att A para 55).

  1. The LCR is 98% on the morning's calculation. What do you do?

Answer: Tell the controller immediately, before distribution. The Example Bank has breached the 100% minimum (APS 210 para 55(a)), so APRA must be informed immediately (Att A para 5; APS 210 para 14), and the contingency funding plan considered (APS 210 paras 47 to 51). Follow document 12. Do not change the number.

  1. A$12m of a customer's deposit has no customer type. What do you do at 08:30, and why?

    Answer: Apply the conservative default (financial institution, non-operational, 100% outflow), log the exception, and ask the data owner. Fix at source, reverse the default and record the effect. Never leave the field blank or assume the favourable answer (document 07 section 10).

What to remember

  • The LCR is counted HQLA divided by net cash outflows over the next 30 days; the minimum is 100% (APS 210 para 55; Att A para 3).
  • Net outflows = outflows minus the lower of inflows and 75% of outflows (Att A para 30). Test the cap daily.
  • Example Bank: HQLA 17.0, outflows 13.45, inflows 1.90, net 11.55, LCR 147.2%, surplus 5.45. Learn these numbers.
  • The numerator is market value, unencumbered, controlled by Treasury, and has no double counting (Att A paras 22, 23, 31).
  • Only fully performing contractual inflows count, at the latest possible date (Att A paras 54 and 60).
  • Explain every move: total change = numerator effect + denominator effect, line by line.
  • Run the forward-looking LCR. Debt rolling into the window is the most predictable cause of a fall in the ratio.
  • Four-eyes always: prepare, review, sign. Never adjust a number to meet a limit.
  • Below 125% amber, below 120% Board appetite, below 100%: tell people immediately, and for 100% inform APRA immediately (Att A para 5).
