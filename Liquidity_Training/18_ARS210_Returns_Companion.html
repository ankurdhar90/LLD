18 ARS 210.0 companion: the liquidity returns, clause by clause and line by line

Purpose: To let you open Reporting Standard ARS 210.0 Liquidity at any clause, or any form item, and find what it means, what you do about it and which line of our own working papers feeds it.

You will be able to:

  • Explain how the reporting standard, the forms and the form instructions fit together, and which of them are rules.
  • Find the training entry for any of the 22 clauses of ARS 210.0.
  • For ARF 210.1A, 210.1B, 210.3.1, 210.3.2, 210.4, 210.5 and 210.6, say what goes in each item, which APS 210 paragraph sits behind it and which working paper line feeds it.
  • Fill a specimen ARF 210.1A and ARF 210.6 for Example Bank and recompute every figure.
  • Apply the validation and reconciliation rules between the forms.
  • State what is confirmed, and what is not, about how the forms are submitted today, ARF 210.5, Level 1 and Level 2, resubmission and any new version.

Before you start: Read document 11 (Returns to APRA and public disclosure), document 08 (Producing the daily LCR) and document 09 (Producing the NSFR). Documents 05 and 06 (the APS 210 companions for Attachments A, B and C) give the rule behind each rate used here.

Time: About five hours to read; keep it open beside the form whenever you prepare a return.

How to read the labels. Sources are numbered [S1], [S2] and so on, and listed at the end. Facts from the registered text of ARS 210.0 are cited as (ARS 210.0 clause 11) or (ARF 210.1A item 8). Rules in APS 210 are cited (APS 210 para 55) or (Att A para 30). Anything not taken from a source I read is labelled Unverified, Typical practice or Interpretation. Document 11 is the overview; this document goes deeper and does not replace it.

1. What the return is, and how the pieces fit together

1.1 In everyday words

Think of the way you complete a tax return. There is a law that says you must file (the Act). There is a form with numbered boxes. There are notes that tell you what goes in each box. And there is a separate rulebook about how to work out the figures. The liquidity return has the same four layers.

Layer What it is The liquidity example Is it a rule?
The Act The law that lets APRA collect data Financial Sector (Collection of Data) Act 2001, section 13 (ARS 210.0 clause 1) Yes
The reporting standard The legal instrument that says who reports, which forms, how often, by when ARS 210.0 Liquidity, clauses 1 to 22 Yes
The reporting forms Grids of numbered items to fill in ARF 210.1A, 210.1B, 210.2, 210.3.1, 210.3.2, 210.4, 210.5, 210.6 Yes, they are part of the standard
The instructions Notes to each form that say what goes in each item, the units and the sign conventions "Instructions" after each form Yes, they are part of the standard
The prudential standard The rulebook for the calculation itself APS 210 Liquidity (Attachments A, B and C) Yes, a separate instrument
The practice guide APRA's guidance on applying APS 210 APG 210 Liquidity No, guidance only

The forms do not say how to work out the LCR. They say where to put the answer. The "weights" in the forms are copied from APS 210 (ARF 210.1A instructions, column 2: "in accordance with the requirements of Attachment A of APS 210"). So you always work in two steps: first classify and weigh under APS 210 (documents 07, 08 and 09), then place the balances in the form.

From the controller: If a form instruction and APS 210 ever seem to disagree, do not pick one. Write down both texts, tell me, and I will raise it with APRA. Sections 10 and 11 show places where this really happens.

1.2 The terms you need first

  • ADI: authorised deposit-taking institution, the legal name for a bank, building society or credit union (clause 21).
  • LCR ADI / MLH ADI: a bank classified under APS 210 para 52 as subject to the liquidity coverage ratio, or to the simpler minimum liquidity holdings (nine per cent) test (clause 21; APS 210 paras 52, 55, 57). Example Bank is an LCR ADI.
  • Level 1 / Level 2: the bank alone, or the whole banking group (APS 210 para 3; clause 21).
  • Reporting form (ARF): "Authorised deposit-taking institution Reporting Form", a numbered grid, for example ARF 210.1A.
  • Derived (shaded) field: a box the form calculates for you. You never type into it (instructions to every form).
  • Weighted amount: the balance multiplied by the rate set in APS 210 (haircut for assets, run-off rate for outflows, inflow rate for inflows).

3. The front of the instrument

3.1 The determination (before the standard)

What it says: Michael Murphy, a delegate of APRA, under paragraph 13(1)(a) of the Financial Sector (Collection of Data) Act 2001 (a) revokes determination No. 4 of 2022, including the ARS 210.0 made under it, and (b) determines ARS 210.0 in the form in the Schedule. Under section 15 of the Act, the new standard begins to apply, and the old one stops, on the day of registration. The instrument commences on registration. It is dated 31 March 2023.

In everyday words: This is the legal "cover page" that makes the standard law and switches off the old one.

What you do about it: Record the instrument number (F2023L00417) in the return version log. Nothing else.

Evidence we keep: The version log entry and a copy of the instrument from legislation.gov.au saved with the quarter's return file.

3.2 Objective of the reporting standard

What it says: The standard sets out the requirements for providing APRA with information about an ADI's liquidity and funding. It includes forms and instructions and "should be read in conjunction with" APS 210.

In everyday words: The standard is the delivery rulebook, APS 210 is the calculation rulebook.

What you do about it: Always have both open when you prepare a form.

Evidence we keep: The return checklist records the APS 210 version (July 2025, F2025L00653) and the ARS 210.0 version used.

4. Clause by clause

The standard has 22 numbered clauses. Each has an entry below, in order. Document 11 explains the same clauses by theme; here you have them one at a time.

ARS 210.0 clause 1: Authority

What it says: The standard is made under section 13 of the Financial Sector (Collection of Data) Act 2001.

In everyday words: The Act gives APRA the power to demand data. This is where the power is cited.

What you do about it: Nothing day to day. It matters because giving false or misleading information to APRA is an offence (see the penalty warning in APRA Connect Guide section 1 "Penalties for False Information", which refers to the Criminal Code) [S9].

Evidence we keep: The attestation and sign-off record for each return (clause 16).

ARS 210.0 clause 2: Purpose

What it says: Information collected is used by APRA for prudential supervision, including assessing compliance with APS 210. It may also be used by the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA).

In everyday words: APRA reads these numbers to check we meet the LCR and NSFR, and it can share them with the central bank.

What you do about it: Treat every figure as one that APRA and the RBA may compare with every other bank, and with what we publish under APS 330 (document 11, section 8).

Evidence we keep: None specific. The reconciliations in section 9 show the numbers agree with our own records.

ARS 210.0 clause 3: Application

What it says: The standard applies to all ADIs excluding providers of purchased payment facilities. It may also apply to the non-operating holding company (NOHC) of an ADI (refer to clause 6).

In everyday words: Every bank has to report, apart from a small group of payment-facility providers. A holding company above the bank may be the one that reports for the group.

What you do about it: Example Bank is an ADI and has no NOHC, so it reports itself.

Evidence we keep: The licence and classification record showing Example Bank as an LCR ADI (APS 210 para 52).

ARS 210.0 clause 4: Commencement

What it says: The standard applies for reporting periods ending on or after 31 March 2023.

In everyday words: The first quarter this version covered was the one ended 31 March 2023.

What you do about it: No action. It tells you that any earlier period was reported under the older standard (see clause 20).

Evidence we keep: The version log.

ARS 210.0 clause 5: Level 1 information

What it says: An ADI to which the standard applies must give APRA the information designated for an ADI at Level 1, as set out in clause 8, for each reporting period.

In everyday words: Every reporting ADI sends the "bank alone" set of forms.

What you do about it: Prepare each form at Level 1. For Example Bank the Level 1 figures equal the Level 2 figures (APS 210 para 3; document 04).

Evidence we keep: One set of forms labelled Level 1 in the return file.

ARS 210.0 clause 6: Level 2 information, and the NOHC

What it says: If an ADI is part of a Level 2 group, it must also give the Level 2 information in clause 8 for each reporting period, unless it is a subsidiary of an authorised NOHC. If it is, its immediate parent NOHC must give the Level 2 information. The NOHC must comply with the standard, other than clause 5, as if it were the ADI.

In everyday words: A bank with subsidiaries sends a second, group-wide set. If a holding company sits above it, the holding company sends that group set instead.

What you do about it: Example Bank has no subsidiaries and no NOHC. Whether it must also lodge a separate Level 2 set is answered in section 8.

Evidence we keep: The group structure chart used to decide the reporting levels, reviewed each year.

ARS 210.0 clause 7: ADIs that are neither LCR nor MLH

What it says: An ADI that is neither an LCR ADI nor an MLH ADI provides information only on receiving written notice from APRA.

In everyday words: Only banks classified under APS 210 report routinely.

What you do about it: No action for Example Bank.

Evidence we keep: None.

ARS 210.0 clause 8: Forms by class of ADI

What it says: An ADI completes a separate form for each reporting consolidation level listed for its class. The table gives: for an LCR ADI that is locally incorporated, Level 1 and Level 2 for ARF 210.1A, 210.1B, 210.3.1, 210.3.2, 210.4, 210.5 and 210.6; for a foreign LCR ADI the domestic books for the same forms except 210.6; for an MLH ADI (local, Level 1 and Level 2; foreign, domestic books) ARF 210.2, 210.3.1, 210.3.2, 210.4 and 210.5. A note says 210.6 is only for locally incorporated LCR ADIs.

In everyday words: The class of bank decides the forms. Example Bank gets seven of the eight.

What you do about it: Keep a form register listing the seven forms, their levels, due dates and preparers.

Evidence we keep: The form register, reviewed each time APRA notifies a change (clauses 18 and 19).

ARS 210.0 clause 9: Forms and method of submission

What it says: Subject to clause 10, the information must be given in electronic format using an electronic method on APRA's website or a method notified by APRA before submission.

In everyday words: You must send the return electronically, by the route APRA tells you.

What you do about it: Submit through the route in section 8, not by email or paper.

Evidence we keep: The submission confirmation (APRA Connect sends a confirmation email with a submission reference to the Regulatory Reporting Administrators) [S9].

ARS 210.0 clause 10: ARF 210.5 method

What it says: The information for ARF 210.5 must be given in an electronic form as notified by APRA at the time of its request.

In everyday words: For the daily report, APRA tells you how to send it when it asks.

What you do about it: When a request arrives, record the method it names and follow it.

Evidence we keep: The written request from APRA and our reply.

ARS 210.0 clause 11: Reporting periods and due dates

What it says: Subject to clauses 12, 13 and 14, information is provided for each quarter based on the ADI's financial year under the Corporations Act 2001, within 35 calendar days of the end of the quarter. ARF 210.4 is provided annually within 35 calendar days of the ADI's end of financial year.

In everyday words: Quarterly forms are due 35 days after each quarter end, and the funding plan 35 days after the financial year end. Calendar days include weekends.

What you do about it: Diary the dates at the start of the year. On a 30 June year end the dates are 4 November, 4 February, 5 May and 4 August (arithmetic from the clause; document 11, section 3.5). Aim to submit well before.

Evidence we keep: The diary, and the submission confirmation showing the date and time.

ARS 210.0 clause 12: The daily liquidity report

What it says: A completed ARF 210.5 is due by close of business on (a) the same day (data at close of business the previous day) that the ADI receives APRA's request, or (b) the next business day (data at close of business on the date of the request) if the request was received after 12 pm. After a request the ADI must keep providing it daily for the number of consecutive business days, or other duration or frequency, that APRA specifies.

In everyday words: If APRA asks, you have hours, not days, and you keep going until APRA says stop.

What you do about it: Keep an ARF 210.5 "ready-to-run" pack (document 10, section 7) and test it. See section 8 for the open question on monthly reporting.

Evidence we keep: The request, the time it arrived, the submissions and confirmations.

ARS 210.0 clause 13: APRA can vary the reporting periods

What it says: APRA may by written notice vary the reporting periods for a particular ADI to require information more or less often, having regard to (a) the ADI's circumstances, (b) how far the information is needed for prudential supervision and (c) the RBA's requirements.

In everyday words: APRA can tell one bank to report monthly, or daily, even though the standard says quarterly.

What you do about it: Keep every APRA notice of this kind in the form register. If one exists for ARF 210.5, it is the likely explanation of the "monthly return" (section 8).

Evidence we keep: The notice, and the form register entry showing the new frequency and its start date.

ARS 210.0 clause 14: APRA can extend the due date

What it says: APRA may by written notice extend a due date. The new due date is the one on the notice.

In everyday words: Only APRA can give more time, and only in writing.

What you do about it: Never assume an extension. If we expect to be late, tell the controller at once (document 12, late return procedure).

Evidence we keep: The extension notice, if any.

ARS 210.0 clause 15: External auditor review

What it says: All information provided must come from systems, processes and controls reviewed and tested by the external auditor as set out in APS 310 Audit and Related Matters. The review is annual or more often if the auditor requires.

In everyday words: The auditor tests the machinery that makes the return.

What you do about it: Keep the controls in document 12 running and evidenced so that the auditor can test them.

Evidence we keep: Reconciliations, review sign-offs, change logs and the auditor's walkthrough notes.

ARS 210.0 clause 16: Internal review and authorisation

What it says: All information must be subject to systems, processes and controls developed by the ADI for internal review and authorisation, to assure completeness and reliability.

In everyday words: We need our own checks and sign-off before anything leaves the bank.

What you do about it: Follow the four-eyes rule (Example Bank convention): one prepares, a second reviews, the controller signs.

Evidence we keep: The signed return checklist, validation report and review notes.

ARS 210.0 clause 17: Digital signature

What it says: When an officer or agent submits information using a method notified by APRA, they must digitally sign it using a digital certificate acceptable to APRA.

In everyday words: The person who submits must be identified electronically.

What you do about it: See section 8: APRA's current pages describe APRA Connect sign-in through myID and Relationship Authorisation Manager rather than a certificate [S9]. Whether that satisfies clause 17 for the ARS 210 forms is not stated on the pages I read.

Unverified: I found no APRA page that says how clause 17 is met under APRA Connect. Ask your controller to confirm with APRA's data team and record the answer.

Evidence we keep: The user access record for each submitter and the role assignments.

ARS 210.0 clause 18: Minor variations

What it says: APRA may make minor variations to a form to correct technical, programming or logical errors, inconsistencies or anomalies, or to the instructions to clarify them, without changing any substantive requirement.

In everyday words: APRA can fix typos and bugs in the forms without a new standard.

What you do about it: On each notice, update our form template and mapping and re-test.

Evidence we keep: The notice and the change log.

ARS 210.0 clause 19: Notice of minor variations

What it says: If APRA makes such a variation it will notify each reporting ADI in writing.

In everyday words: You will be told, in writing.

What you do about it: Make sure APRA's correspondence reaches a monitored shared mailbox and not one person's inbox.

Evidence we keep: The notices.

ARS 210.0 clause 20: Transition

What it says: An ADI must report under the old reporting standard for a "transitional reporting period": a period that ended before the old standard was revoked, where the report was due on or after revocation. A note says overdue documents under the old standard are still owed under it.

In everyday words: The new standard did not rewrite the past.

What you do about it: No action now. It is the pattern to expect whenever APRA issues a new version.

Evidence we keep: The version log.

ARS 210.0 clause 21: Interpretation (definitions)

What it says: The clause defines more than 100 terms used in the forms. Many point back to APS 210 (for example HQLA1 "has the meaning given in paragraph 9 of Attachment A of Prudential Standard APS 210"). Terms that appear in bold italics in the instructions are defined here.

In everyday words: The clause is the dictionary for the forms. A word in the instructions may not mean what it means in conversation.

What you do about it: Look the word up before classifying. The table in section 4.1 lists the definitions you will use most.

Evidence we keep: The classification rules document (document 07) records the definition relied on for each rule.

ARS 210.0 clause 22: References to other instruments

What it says: Unless the contrary intention appears, a reference to an Act, prudential standard, reporting standard or accounting or auditing standard is to the instrument as in force from time to time.

In everyday words: "APS 210" means the current APS 210, not the one in force in 2023.

What you do about it: Read the form instructions against today's APS 210. That is why some paragraph numbers in the instructions are out of date (section 11).

Evidence we keep: The record of the APS 210 version used.

6. The maturity and plan forms: ARF 210.3.1, ARF 210.3.2 and ARF 210.4

6.1 What these forms are for

The two contractual maturity mismatch forms show, for every balance sheet line, when the cash is contractually due. Put together, they show where cash coming in and going out do not match in time. They support the maturity mismatch analysis and monitoring that APS 210 requires (APS 210 para 36; document 10, section 2). ARF 210.4 then looks forward three years. It is the numbers behind the funding strategy (APS 210 paras 44 to 46; document 10, section 6).

From the controller: Of all the liquidity forms these are the least forgiving, because they are contractual. You may not apply behavioural assumptions. A mortgage book does not become "five years" because customers usually repay early. It goes where the contract puts it, except in the specific memo items the instructions name.

8.1 How are the quarterly ARS 210 forms submitted today, and from which reporting period?

What is confirmed.

  • The standard itself only says the data go "in electronic format using an electronic method available on APRA's website or by a method notified by APRA prior to submission" (ARS 210.0 clause 9) [S1].
  • APRA Connect is the portal. Version 9.1 of the APRA Connect Guide (released May 2026) says users sign in with myID through the Relationship Authorisation Manager; the ADI collections accept XML or XLSX uploads; returns that pass validation are submitted automatically on upload, or are submitted manually with "Validate & Submit"; and on receipt APRA emails a confirmation with a submission reference to the entity's Regulatory Reporting Administrators [S9].
  • APRA's "Migrating D2A Collections Support Material" page lists, among the ADI collections: "ADI Daily Liquidity (ARF 210.5)", with reference-period reporting in APRA Connect beginning 31 May 2026; and "ADI – Liquidity", available in the external test environment in August 2026, with reference-period reporting in APRA Connect beginning 30 September 2026. It lists a "Liquidity Taxonomy" and an "ADI Liquidity Upload Template", both dated 26 August 2026, and a taxonomy and upload template for the Daily Liquidity Report dated 10 June 2026 [S12].
  • APRA's notice of 27 July 2026, "End of Alternate Submission Process for ADIs and RFCs", makes APRA Connect mandatory from: "Liquidity 210.5 monthly return", 31 July 2026; EFS, 31 August 2026; "All other ADI reporting collections that have migrated to APRA Connect", 30 September 2026. Footnote 1 to the last row says it "Excludes forms for ARS 210 - Liquidity, ARS 325 - International Operations, ARS 910 - Financial Claims Scheme, ARF 923.1 - Quarterly Provisioning, and ARF 923.5 - Residential Mortgage Information Request" [S11].
  • The "Alternative submission arrangements" page lists Excel templates for ADI and RFC EFS returns and says APRA will contact entities near their due dates with tailored guidance. It lists no liquidity-specific template [S13].

So, from these pages, APRA Connect is the planned route and the bank may use it, but the date from which the alternate route (D2A and its Excel templates) stops being available for the quarterly forms is not stated.

8.3 Does a single-entity ADI lodge Level 1 only, or Level 1 and Level 2?

What the text says.

  • Clause 5: every ADI provides the Level 1 information. Clause 6: an ADI that "is part of a Level 2 group" must also provide the Level 2 information, unless it is a subsidiary of an authorised NOHC (clause 6) [S1].
  • The ARF 210.1A and 210.1B instructions: locally incorporated ADIs complete both forms at Level 1 and Level 2, and "For ADIs without a Level 2 consolidation ARF 210.1B is to be completed at Level 1" [S1]. The other forms' instructions say "at Level 1 and Level 2 for locally incorporated ADIs".
  • APRA's standard page is titled "ARS 210.0 Liquidity (Level 1 and Level 2)" [S3].
  • APRA's migration FAQ: for ARF 210.5, "remove any reporting consolidations that do not apply to your entity" [S14].

Interpretation. An ADI with no subsidiaries has no Level 2 group, so clause 6 does not create a separate Level 2 duty, and the specific sentence in the ARF 210.1B instructions says such an ADI completes the form at Level 1. The most natural reading is that Example Bank lodges Level 1 only. The FAQ on ARF 210.5 supports the idea that a consolidation that does not apply is simply left out.

Unverified. I found no APRA statement that says in terms that a single-entity ADI lodges Level 1 only for the other forms, nor a definition of "Level 2 group" for a one-entity ADI (it is in APS 001 Definitions, which I did not read). The sentence quoted from the ARF 210.1B instructions refers only to that form. What to do: look at what Example Bank has lodged in past quarters. 8.4 APRA's resubmission process

What is confirmed (APRA Connect Guide v9.1, May 2026, section 7.4) [S9].

  1. "If changes are required after submitting a regulatory return, a resubmission can be requested via APRA Connect and these requests are automatically approved." Requests for non-regulatory returns (ad hoc submissions) are reviewed and approved.
  2. To request: on the Submission History page click the Resubmission icon (or open the return and select Request Resubmission); choose the reason for resubmission from the list; add details; summarise changes since the previous submission in the second text field; attach a supporting file if needed; click Send Request, then Confirm.
  3. If the return meets the criteria for automatic approval it leaves the submission history and goes back to the draft returns list. You correct it and submit again. Confirmation notifications for resubmitted returns carry the same receipt number and differ by timestamp.
  4. Ad hoc returns are not used to request a resubmission of a regulatory return (section 6.11.3 of the guide). The guide says, for the standard reporting cycle: "A request for resubmission is required for all other returns containing erroneous or incomplete data".
  5. Validation: errors block submission; warnings need a comment; cross-form, cross-return and cross-period validation exist; the guide uses bankers' rounding (x.5 rounds to the nearest even number) [S9].
  6. Under D2A, "Historical resubmissions" for earlier periods are submitted as an "Ad hoc file upload" return, as one ZIP file named D2A_resubmission_<return_name>_<period_end>.zip, and a D2A resubmission is not complete until APRA processes the data successfully [S13].
  7. On a recent liquidity item APRA said "Resubmission of historical liquidity reporting will not be required" for the settlement service provider FAQ (letter of 2 June 2026) [S16]. That is a statement about one FAQ, not a general rule.

Unverified. I found no ARS 210-specific resubmission rule, no deadline after which a resubmission needs review, no APRA statement of when an error must be notified, and no statement of the materiality at which APRA expects a correction for liquidity returns. (An APRA response on economic and financial statistics mentions accuracy thresholds for that standard only; that is not ARS 210.) What to do: follow the procedure in document 11, section 6.12 and also tell the controller, who decides whether APRA's supervisor must be told before the resubmission request.

11. A note on "paragraph 70 of APS 210" in ARF 210.1A item 29.1

What the form says. Item 29.1, "Net cash outflows overlay", is described in the instructions as the amount "of any adjustments applied to net cash outflows that are specific to the application of the requirements of paragraph 70 of APS 210". If APRA has not advised an overlay, report zero.

Where the number came from. APRA's response to submissions of 10 December 2020 explains that a "net cash flow overlay item" was added to ARF 210.1A and ARF 210.1B to capture adjustments APRA makes to net cash outflows under paragraph 70 of APS 210, "such as those announced in October and December 2020" [S6]. So in the APS 210 in force in 2020, paragraph 70 was the paragraph that gave APRA this power. The reference was carried unchanged into the 2023 reporting standard.

What the current standard says. In APS 210 July 2025 (F2025L00653) paragraph 70 is "Exceptional Liquidity Assistance": an ADI under severe stress may ask the RBA, after telling APRA immediately, and must be operationally able to provide information. Paragraph 56 says "APRA may require an LCR ADI to maintain a higher minimum LCR if APRA has concerns about the ADI's liquidity risk profile or the quality of its liquidity risk management", and paragraph 71 allows APRA to adjust or exclude a requirement for an ADI.

Interpretation. The numbering in the form is out of date. An APRA-advised overlay today would most likely rest on paragraph 56 (a higher minimum LCR) or paragraph 71 (adjustment of a requirement). This is my reading; Unverified: I found no APRA page that links item 29.1 to a current paragraph.

What you do about it.

  1. Enter zero in item 29.1 unless APRA has told Example Bank in writing about an adjustment to net cash outflows.
  2. If APRA does, record the letter, the amount, the date it starts and the date it ends, and enter the amount as APRA states.
  3. Do not read the paragraph 70 reference in the form as a request for ELA information; that duty (to be operationally able to provide information when requesting ELA) is separate and sits with the controller and Treasury (document 11, section 7; APS 210 para 70).
  4. Ask your controller to confirm the reference with APRA's data team and note the answer in the form register.

Evidence we keep: APRA's letter, if any; the written confirmation that item 29.1 is zero.

Check yourself

  1. Which clause sets the due date for the quarterly forms, and what is the due date for the quarter ended 31 December? Answer: ARS 210.0 clause 11 sets 35 calendar days after quarter end. 31 December plus 35 days is 4 February (31 January is 31 days after 31 December; 4 more days gives 4 February).
  2. An LCR ADI has HQLA of A$17,000.0 million, total weighted outflows of A$13,450.0 million and weighted inflows of A$12,000.0 million. What do items 28, 29 and 30 show? Answer: Item 28 is the lower of 12,000.0 and 75% x 13,450.0 = 10,087.5, so 10,087.5. Item 29 = 13,450.0 + 0.0 - 10,087.5 = 3,362.5. Item 30 = 17,000.0 / 3,362.5 x 100 = 505.6.
  3. Adjusted HQLA1 is A$60.0m and adjusted HQLA2A (after the 15% haircut) is A$85.0m, with no HQLA2B. Find items 4.1, 4.2 and 4. Answer: Item 4.1: maximum of [0 - 15/85 x (60 + 85)], [0 - 15/60 x 60], 0 = 0. Item 4.2: maximum of [(85 + 0 - 0) - 2/3 x 60], 0 = 85 - 40 = 45. Item 4 = 60 + 85 + 0 - 0 - 45 = 100.
  4. Example Bank lends A$3.0bn of its RBA balance as standard mortgages (LVR 80% or below, over one year). How do items 21, 35.1 and the NSFR change? Answer: Item 21 falls by 3,000.0 at 0% RSF (no change). Item 35.1 rises by 3,000.0 x 65% = 1,950.0. Total RSF = 58,500.0 + 1,950.0 = 60,450.0. NSFR = 79,750.0 / 60,450.0 = 131.9%.
  5. In which ARF 210.6 item do non-HQLA debt securities held for three years go, and at what factor? Answer: Item 38 (item 38.1 if unencumbered), in the one-year-or-more column at 85% (ARF 210.6 item 38).
  6. In ARF 210.5, how are an inflow of margin received and a loan repayment entered? Answer: Inflows are negative numbers and outflows are positive (ARF 210.5 "Specific instructions"), so margin received and loan repayments (principal inflows) are entered as negative numbers.
  7. What does the form instruction for item 29.1 get wrong today, and what do you enter if APRA has not written to the bank? Answer: It refers to "paragraph 70 of APS 210", which in July 2025 is Exceptional Liquidity Assistance; the older numbering is out of date. Enter zero.
  8. Example Bank's on-balance sheet liabilities are A$92.0bn. What size of exposure to one connected group must be listed in ARF 210.3.2 item 10? Answer: More than 1.0% of A$92.0bn = A$0.92bn, so more than A$920 million.
  9. A return is found to contain an error after submission through APRA Connect. What is the first step? Answer: Request a resubmission through APRA Connect (Submission History, Request Resubmission, choose the reason, summarise the changes, send). Regulatory return requests are automatically approved; correct and resubmit. Tell the controller first, as the notification decision is theirs.
  10. A return shows unencumbered ARF 210.3.1 items 2.1.1 and 2.1.2 of A$12.5bn but ARF 210.1A items 1.3.1 and 1.3.2 of A$12.0bn. Name two possible reasons. Answer: First, the valuation basis: ARF 210.3.1 uses carrying amounts and ARF 210.1A uses market value. Second, eligibility: ARF 210.1A includes only securities that meet the operational requirements for HQLA (Att A paras 22 to 25), while ARF 210.3.1 reports all unencumbered securities. What to remember

  • ARS 210.0 (F2023L00417, in force from 4 April 2023, no amendments or consultation found) is the delivery rulebook. APS 210 is the calculation rulebook. The forms' instructions are part of the reporting standard and are rules.
  • Quarterly forms are due 35 calendar days after quarter end; ARF 210.4 is annual, 35 days after the financial year end; ARF 210.5 is on request, with APRA's own pages now describing a monthly collection. APRA alone can extend a due date, in writing (clauses 11 to 14).
  • Example Bank files ARF 210.1A, 210.1B, 210.3.1, 210.3.2, 210.4, 210.5 and 210.6 and does not file ARF 210.2.
  • You report balances; the form applies the weights. Specimen results: LCR 147.2% (11,550.0 net outflows against 17,000.0 HQLA) and NSFR 136.3% (79,750.0 ASF against 58,500.0 RSF).
  • The maturity forms are contractual; ARF 210.5 uses outflows positive and inflows negative; all other forms use positive numbers.
  • Routes and dates for submission, the monthly ARF 210.5 and the Level 1 versus Level 2 question are only partly confirmed. Record the facts from our last confirmation and ask APRA's data team in writing.
  • Resubmission goes through APRA Connect and is automatically approved for regulatory returns, but the controller decides who is told.
  • Several form references use old APS 210 numbering (item 29.1 cites paragraph 70). Follow the substance.
