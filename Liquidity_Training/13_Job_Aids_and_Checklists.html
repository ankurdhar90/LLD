13 Job aids and checklists

Purpose: To give you print-and-use cards, checklists and templates for the everyday work of the Liquidity Reporting team, so you can run each task correctly without searching the standard each time.

You will be able to:

  • run the daily liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) and month-end net stable funding ratio (NSFR) with a checklist in hand;
  • review a colleague's work with a standard list of checks;
  • classify any common product by asking the right questions and reading off the rate or factor;
  • look up any run-off rate, inflow rate, haircut, available stable funding (ASF) factor or required stable funding (RSF) factor on one page;
  • hand over your work and find the right person to ask;
  • answer the 25 questions new analysts ask most.

Before you start: Read documents 02 to 09 first. These aids are quick-reference tools, not substitutes for understanding. Where an aid and the standard differ, the standard wins. The standard is Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity, July 2025 (APS 210).

Time: Nothing to read in one sitting. Use each aid when you need it. Allow 30 minutes to find your way around this document the first time.

From the controller: Print the cards in sections 7 to 13 and keep them at your desk for your first three months. After that you will not need them for the common cases, but you will be glad of them for the odd ones.

1. Daily LCR checklist

Trigger: each business day. Output: a signed LCR for the previous business day by 10:00 am (Example Bank convention). Owner: preparer; review: second person; sign: controller. Evidence: the completed checklist, working paper, reconciliations and movement analysis saved in the day's folder.

Example Bank is a single legal entity in one currency, so Level 1 equals Level 2 and the Australian dollar (AUD) LCR equals the all-currencies LCR. Both must be at least 100% (APS 210 para 55).

# Step Check Done
1 Confirm all source feeds arrived (deposits, loans, treasury system, collateral, ledger, customer master) Feed log complete; any late feed escalated to the controller by 09:00 [ ]
2 Run record-count and balance reconciliation from each source to the extract No missing accounts; differences explained [ ]
3 Run validation rules Exception list worked to zero or accepted in writing; defaulted records listed (conservative category used) [ ]
4 Reconcile to the general ledger Total assets and total liabilities and equity agree to the GL; difference nil or explained [ ]
5 Value the liquid asset portfolio at market value (Att A para 9) Prices from the agreed source; no stale prices [ ]
6 Confirm HQLA eligibility Each security checked against Att A paras 9 to 12 (see card 9) [ ]

8 Check any asset that has become ineligible On the grace-period register; day count correct (Att A para 25) [ ]
9 Apply haircuts HQLA1 0%; HQLA2A 15%; HQLA2B 25% or 50% (Att A paras 9 to 12) [ ]
10 Apply the caps HQLA2 no more than 40% of the stock; HQLA2B no more than 15% (Att A paras 6 to 8) [ ]
11 Classify and weight outflows Rates from the locked table (card 10); scorecard applied to less stable deposits (Att A para 40) [ ]

7. Classification quick cards

Each card is for one product. Ask the questions in order. Read the LCR category and rate, and the NSFR category and factor, from the outcome table. If a field you need is blank, take the conservative category (the one with the higher run-off, or the lower inflow, or the higher RSF, or the lower ASF), list the record as defaulted and tell the controller. The complete rate lists are on cards 10 and 11. Document 07 (Data) explains the reasoning.

Card 7.1 Retail deposits at call (natural persons)

Questions

  1. Is the depositor a natural person (an individual)? If not, it is wholesale: use cards 7.3 to 7.6 (Att A para 34).
  2. Did the money come through an intermediary (a platform or fund)? If so, go to card 7.7 (Att A para 35).
  3. Is the deposit fully covered by the Financial Claims Scheme (A$250,000 per account holder per ADI)? And is either (a) another established relationship with the bank in place that makes withdrawal highly unlikely, or (b) the account a transaction account (for example salary credited)? If both parts are yes, it is stable (Att A para 37).
  4. If not, it is less stable (Att A para 38): score it on the scorecard (card 8).

Card 7.2 Retail term deposits

Questions

  1. Residual maturity or withdrawal notice period of 30 days or less? Treat like an at-call deposit (card 7.1), because it can leave inside the window.
  2. More than 30 days: does the depositor have no legal right to withdraw within 30 days, or does early withdrawal carry a significant penalty that is materially greater than the loss of interest? If yes, the outflow is excluded (0%) (Att A para 41).
  3. Does the bank in practice allow withdrawal despite the clause? If yes, the whole category is treated as demand deposits (Att A para 42). Hardship withdrawals under a defined hardship policy do not change the treatment of the pool (Att A para 43).
  4. Residual maturity of one year or more? NSFR 100%.

Card 7.6 Deposits from financial institutions and superannuation funds

The practice case (document 14, line j) is the model: at-call deposits from superannuation funds are financial institution deposits (APS 210 para 10), not operational, so they run off at 100% and give 0% ASF.

Questions

  1. Is the depositor a financial institution as defined? (APS 210 para 10)
  2. Is it a genuine operational deposit under card 7.4? Only then 5% or 25%, and 50% ASF.
  3. Otherwise: 100% LCR; ASF by maturity as in row C of card 7.5.
  4. Is it a deposit between ADIs within a cooperative network? Special ASF treatment needs APRA approval (Att C para 15).

Card 7.7 Intermediated deposits (platforms, brokers, funds)

Questions (Att A para 35). Treat the deposit as the person's own only if, during the next 30 days, all four are true:

  1. the person keeps all legal rights over withdrawal or movement of the funds;
  2. the person exercises those rights in practice and cannot transfer them to the intermediary;
  3. there is clear and prominent disclosure to the person that the funds will be placed with the ADI;
  4. the intermediary or an associated entity cannot make investment decisions for the person or withdraw funds without the person's specific direction (apart from fees, expense reimbursements and taxes).

You must be able to demonstrate how the treatment meets the conditions.

Card 7.13 Business, personal and other loans (not to financial institutions)

Questions

  1. Customer: retail or SME; non-financial corporate; sovereign or PSE? (If it is a financial institution use card 7.14.)
  2. Residual maturity: the latest possible date, with extension options assumed exercised (Att C para 20). No defined maturity: treat as one year or more (Att C para 21).
  3. Does it qualify for a 35% or lower risk weight under APS 112 and is it one year or more? RSF 65% (Att C para 35). Other performing loans of one year or more: 85% (Att C para 36).

6 Escalate by level: Amber, Liquidity Risk and Treasury the same day. Red, plus CFO, CRO and ALCO chair within 2 hours of sign-off. Critical, plus CEO and Board chair, and APRA notified immediately by the authorised person (Att A para 5; APS 210 para 14).
7 Keep a log with times: who knew, who decided, what was sent.
8 Do not speak to APRA, the RBA, the media or customers unless told to.
9 Prepare: movement analysis, shortfall, forward projection, list of maturities, contingency funding plan source table.
10 Update at the agreed times. Close the incident in writing. Report and run the lessons-learned review within 5 business days.

Shortfall formulas. LCR: net cash outflows less HQLA (to 100%); net cash outflows x 1.20 less HQLA (to 120%). NSFR: RSF less ASF (to 100%).

Worked example (practice case incident). HQLA 15.0; outflows 15.44 + 2.8 = 18.24; inflows 2.40; net 15.84; LCR 94.7%; shortfall 0.84. Raise funding of at least A$0.84bn with a term beyond 30 days and hold it at the RBA: A$1.0bn gives HQLA 16.0 and LCR 101.0%.

Contacts to find first (roles): controller; Head of Liquidity Risk; Treasurer; CFO; CRO; the person authorised to notify APRA; Technology on-call.

17. The 25 questions new analysts ask most

1. What is the LCR and what is the minimum? It compares the stock of HQLA with net cash outflows over the next 30 calendar days in a severe stress (Att A para 3). A locally incorporated ADI must keep both the AUD LCR and the all-currencies LCR at 100% or more (APS 210 para 55). Example Bank has one currency, so the two are the same number.

2. What is the NSFR and what is the minimum? It is ASF divided by RSF, looking one year ahead (Att C para 3; Att C para 1). A locally incorporated LCR ADI must keep it at 100% or more at all times (APS 210 para 60).

3. What counts as HQLA? HQLA1 (no haircut, no limit), HQLA2A (15% haircut) and HQLA2B (25% or 50% haircut), subject to caps (Att A paras 9 to 12). In Australian dollars, per APRA's liquidity FAQ, only notes and coin, RBA balances and Australian Government and semi-government securities are HQLA1, and nothing qualifies as HQLA2 at present.

4. Why are bonds pledged under a repo not in the stock? Eligible assets must be unencumbered and not pledged to secure or collateralise a transaction (Att A para 23). They are not available to be sold or repoed again, so they cannot count.

5. What is the difference between a stable and a less stable deposit? A stable deposit is fully covered by the Financial Claims Scheme and either the customer has other established relationships with the bank or the account is a transaction account (Att A para 37). Everything else in retail is less stable (Att A para 38).

6. How do I score a less stable deposit? Add 2 if the balance is above the guarantee limit (A$250,000), 2 if on-line, 1 if there is no established relationship and 1 if heavily rate-driven (Att A para 40). A total of 0 to 2 gives 10% run-off and 3 or more gives 25% (Att A Table 2).

7. Why do some retail term deposits over 30 days run off at 0%? Because outflows are excluded if the depositor has no legal right to withdraw within 30 days, or early withdrawal carries a significant penalty materially greater than the lost interest (Att A para 41). If the bank lets customers break them anyway, the whole category must be treated as demand deposits (Att A para 42).

8. What is an operational deposit? A deposit a customer needs to keep with the bank for clearing, custody or cash management, under a binding agreement with at least 30 days' notice or significant switching costs (Att A paras 47 to 48). It must be a by-product of the service, in a designated account, without an incentive to leave excess funds (Att A para 49). Excess balances do not qualify (Att A para 50).

9. Why do superannuation fund deposits run off at 100%? The trustee of a superannuation fund is a financial institution (APS 210 para 10). Non-operational deposits from financial institutions are in the "all other non-operational deposits" row at 100% (Att A Table 3).

10. What is the inflow cap? Inflows can offset at most 75% of outflows, so net outflows can never fall below 25% of outflows (Att A para 30). Example: outflows 100 and inflows 90 gives net outflows of 25.

11. Why are retail and SME inflows only 50%? The bank is assumed to receive all fully performing contractual inflows but to keep lending to retail and SME customers at 50% of those inflows, so the net inflow is 50% (Att A para 61).

12. How do I treat undrawn committed facilities? Apply the run-off rate for the customer type and the facility type (credit or liquidity) to the undrawn portion (Att A Table 3). Facilities to hedge funds, money market funds and funding vehicles are 100% in full (Att A para 52).

13. Why is a corporate revolving facility a credit facility, not a liquidity facility? General working capital facilities must not be classified as liquidity facilities (Att A para 51). A liquidity facility is a back-up for a customer's debt maturing within 30 days; any spare capacity is a credit facility (Att A para 51).

14. When does our own wholesale debt enter the LCR? When its earliest contractual maturity or call date falls within 30 days, or it has an option exercisable by the investor within 30 days (Att A para 45). It then runs off at 100% (Att A Table 3). This is why we run a forward-looking view.

15. What is the HQLA2 cap? HQLA2 may be no more than 40% of the stock and HQLA2B no more than 15% (Att A para 6). In effect adjusted HQLA2 cannot exceed two-thirds of adjusted HQLA1 (Att A para 7).

16. What if an asset stops being eligible? It may stay in the stock for 30 more calendar days (Att A para 25). Log the date, show the LCR with and without it, and make sure Treasury replaces it in time.

17. How do I treat a loan with no maturity for the NSFR? As residual maturity of one year or more (Att C para 21). The relevant RSF factor then follows from the loan type.

18. Which maturity date do I use for the NSFR? For liabilities, the earliest date the funds can be redeemed (Att C para 8). For assets, the latest possible date, assuming extension options are exercised (Att C para 20).

19. What RSF factor applies to an encumbered asset? 100% if encumbered for one year or more; at least 50% (or the higher unencumbered factor) for 6 months to under one year; the unencumbered factor if under 6 months remain (Att C para 23).

20. What are Level 1 and Level 2, and why is the AUD LCR the same as the all-currencies LCR for us? Level 1 is the ADI alone; Level 2 is the consolidated group (APS 210 para 3). Example Bank is one legal entity with one currency, so Level 1 equals Level 2 and the AUD and all-currencies LCRs are the same.

21. When must APRA be told about a liquidity problem? Immediately for an actual or likely LCR breach (Att A para 5), and immediately for a severe liquidity stress (APS 210 para 14). Other concerns about the current or future position must be raised as soon as possible, with the plans to address them (APS 210 para 14). A named, authorised person makes the notification, not the analyst.

22. What happens if a limit is breached? The ADI must put a plan of action in place to review the exposure and reduce it to within the limit (APS 210 para 37). Senior management must have procedures for exceptions, with escalation and follow-up (APS 210 para 19).

23. What is ELA and what changed in July 2025? Exceptional Liquidity Assistance is emergency lending from the RBA to an ADI under severe liquidity stress; the ADI must notify APRA immediately before approaching the RBA (APS 210 para 70). The July 2025 version also requires the ADI to be operationally able to provide information with the application or within APRA's timeframes (APS 210 para 70).

24. How often must the funding strategy and contingency funding plan be reviewed? The funding strategy: reviewed and approved by the Board at least annually, and updated at least annually, with material changes advised to APRA (APS 210 paras 45 to 46). The contingency funding plan: reviewed and tested at least annually, and approved by the Board at least annually (APS 210 para 51).

25. Why does the standard talk about minimum liquidity holdings if we are an LCR ADI? Attachment B applies to MLH ADIs, which hold 9% of liabilities in specified liquid assets (APS 210 para 57; Att B para 1). Example Bank is classified as an LCR ADI (APS 210 para 52), so it does not apply to us, but you will meet it when reading the standard and in peer comparisons. In the July 2025 version MLH ADIs value liquid assets at market value (Att B para 2).
