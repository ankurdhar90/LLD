Liquidity Training

Training pack for the Liquidity Reporting team, built on APRA Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity (July 2025). Read in order, or use the paragraph index to go from any part of the standard to its training. Background reading: Understanding Bank Liquidity in Australia.

A. Foundations

  • 00 Start here
    Welcome you to the team and show you how to use this training pack.
  • 01 Syllabus and document design
    Set out what you will learn, in what order, and what each document in the pack is designed to contain.
  • 02 Liquidity from zero
    To give you, with no background in banking, accounting or liquidity, the picture and the arithmetic you need before you open any other document in this pack. By the end you will have followed a complete liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) and a complete net stable funding ratio (NSFR) for Example Bank, one line at a time.
  • 03 Your role, the team and the working year
    To tell you what the Liquidity Reporting team is accountable for, who else touches the liquidity numbers and what each of them does, what Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity requires of the Board and senior management, what happens and when across the working year, how your first 90 days will run, and how you are expected to behave when the numbers matter.

B. The standard, paragraph by paragraph

  • 04 APS 210 companion: the main body
    To let you open the main body of APS 210 (Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity, July 2025) at any paragraph and find, straight away, what it says in plain English, what it means for our team, and what proof we keep.
  • 05 APS 210 companion: Attachment A (LCR)
    This is your paragraph-by-paragraph companion to Attachment A of Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity (July 2025, F2025L00653), the part of the standard that defines the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR). Open the standard at any paragraph of Attachment A, find the matching entry here, and learn what it says, what it means in plain words, what the Liquidity Reporting team must do about it, what evidence we keep, and where to read more.
  • 06 APS 210 companion: Attachments B (MLH) and C (NSFR)
    Let you open APS 210 (the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority's liquidity standard) at any paragraph of Attachment B (Minimum liquidity holdings) or Attachment C (Net stable funding ratio) and find, in the same order, a plain-English training entry: what the paragraph says, what it means in everyday words, what our team does about it, what evidence we keep and where to learn more.

C. Doing the work

  • 07 Data: where the numbers come from and how each balance is classified
    To teach you where every number in the liquidity returns starts, what facts the bank must hold about each deposit, loan, security and contract, and how to turn each record into the right LCR run-off or inflow rate and the right NSFR factor. This is the document you will use most when someone asks, "How do we treat this product?"
  • 08 Producing the daily LCR, end to end
    To give you a complete operating procedure for the daily liquidity coverage ratio (LCR), from the data at close of business to the signed and distributed result, with the Example Bank figures worked line by line so that you can check your own work against them.
  • 09 Producing the NSFR, end to end
    Give you a complete, step-by-step procedure for producing Example Bank's monthly Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR), from the first data extract to the signed working paper, the ALCO (asset and liability committee) pack and the feed to the APRA return.
  • 10 Monitoring beyond the two ratios
    To teach you everything the Liquidity Reporting team and its neighbours watch besides the liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) and the net stable funding ratio (NSFR): the maturity ladder, funding concentration, limits and early warning indicators, stress tests, the funding strategy, the contingency funding plan, intraday liquidity, collateral, currency, cost allocation, management information, Level 1 and Level 2, and the minimum liquidity holdings (MLH) regime.

D. Reporting and control

  • 11 Returns to APRA and public disclosure
    To teach you how the bank reports its liquidity position to APRA and to the public: what a regulatory return is, which forms Example Bank files and when, how each part of the LCR and NSFR calculation lands on a form, how we produce, check, sign and submit a return, which notifications and approvals APS 210 requires outside the returns, and how public disclosure under APS 330 works.
  • 12 Controls, incidents and escalation
    To teach you what a control is, how the checks around Example Bank's liquidity numbers fit together, and exactly what to do, who to tell and how fast when something goes wrong, from a small data error to a breach of the 100% liquidity coverage ratio (LCR) minimum.

E. Practice

  • 13 Job aids and checklists
    To give you print-and-use cards, checklists and templates for the everyday work of the Liquidity Reporting team, so you can run each task correctly without searching the standard each time.
  • 14 Exercises and assessments
    To let you practise everything in this pack, from simple arithmetic to a full month-end LCR and NSFR, and to prove to yourself and to your controller that you can do the job on your own.
