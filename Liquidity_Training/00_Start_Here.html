00 Start here

Purpose: Welcome you to the team and show you how to use this training pack. Time: 15 minutes.

Welcome. You have joined the Liquidity Reporting team. Our job is to know, every day, whether the bank could pay what it owes if things went badly, to prove it with numbers, and to report those numbers to the Board and to the regulator, APRA (the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority).

You do not need any banking background to start. This pack assumes none. It begins with what a bank is and ends with you producing the daily numbers, preparing the returns to APRA, and knowing what to do when something goes wrong.

What we are responsible for

We produce How often What it tells us Rule
The Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) Every business day Could we survive 30 days of severe stress using our liquid assets? Must be at least 100% (APS 210 para 55)
The Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) Every month Are our long-term assets funded by money that will stay? Must be at least 100% (APS 210 para 60)
Monitoring reports Daily to monthly Where pressure is building: funding that falls due, large depositors, early warning signs (APS 210 paras 36 to 43)
Returns to APRA As the reporting standard requires The official record of our liquidity position Document 11
Escalations When needed Tells management and APRA quickly when a limit is breached (APS 210 para 14)

Every one of these comes from one rulebook: Prudential Standard APS 210 Liquidity (July 2025). Keep the PDF open while you learn. Documents 04, 05 and 06 follow it paragraph by paragraph, and document 15 is an index: look up any paragraph number and it sends you to the page that explains it.

How to study

  1. Read in the order of the syllabus. Each document says what to read first.
  2. Do the "Check yourself" questions before you look at the answers.
  3. When a document cites a paragraph, such as (Att A para 37), open the PDF at that paragraph and read the original. The standard is the authority; this pack only explains it.
  4. Do the exercises in document 14 at the points the syllabus says. You are ready for real work when you can do the Level 3 practice case alone.
  5. Keep document 13 beside you once you start producing numbers.

The bank you have joined

All examples use Example Bank, a fictional Australian bank with A$100 billion of assets. Its LCR is 147.2% and its NSFR is 136.3%. You will rebuild both numbers yourself, line by line. A second set of figures, the practice case, is used for exercises: there the LCR is 115.0%, and in one scenario it falls to 94.7%, which is a breach you will learn to handle.

Three rules of this team

From the controller: These matter more than any formula.

  1. The number is the number. We never adjust a figure to stay inside a limit. If the ratio is low, we report it low and escalate.
  2. If you cannot evidence it, it did not happen. Every figure traces to source data, a rule in the standard and a reviewer's sign-off.
  3. Bad news travels first. The standard requires the bank to tell APRA "as soon as possible" about liquidity concerns (APS 210 para 14). That starts with you telling me the moment something looks wrong.

A note on what is real and what is illustration

Everything described as a rule cites its paragraph in APS 210. Things a bank decides for itself (internal limits, cut-off times, team names) are labelled "Example Bank convention" or "typical practice"; your real bank's versions will differ, so confirm them. Facts from outside APS 210 that could not be checked against an official source are worded cautiously. This pack is training material, not legal or accounting advice; where it and the standard differ, the standard wins.
